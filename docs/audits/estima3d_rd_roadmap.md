# Estima3D R&D Roadmap

**Companion document to** `legend_aware_extraction_audit.md` (same folder) — the internal audit of 7 real structural-steel PDF drawing sets (262 pages, all born-digital/vector text), produced by direct text-layer scanning and by directly executing the production classifier functions. That audit is the factual starting point for everything below; this document does not re-derive its findings, it researches what they imply, using external sourcing (AISC standards and detailing guidance, buildingSMART/IFC and Tekla/SDS2/Advance Steel/Revit documentation, peer-reviewed document-AI/CV research, and practitioner estimating literature) to build a technically actionable R&D roadmap.

**Methodology note:** sections C–T of this report were produced as five parallel research work-packages, each independently researched and written, then assembled here without altering their findings. Sections A and B are new synthesis written after reading all five, specifically to reconcile and summarize their (independently convergent) conclusions. Every claim throughout carries one of four tags, used consistently across every section: **confirmed** (directly verified — either re-derived from the audit's own direct measurements, or a fetched/quoted primary source), **inferred** (a reasoned synthesis of confirmed evidence, not itself stated verbatim by any one source), **reported, not verified** (a secondary source — a blog, a search-engine summary, a paper abstract that could not be fully opened — not independently cross-checked), or **reasoned proposal** (this report's own architecture/process design, internally consistent with the evidence but not itself a measured fact). Internal references (`§D`, `§H`, etc.) point at the lettered sections of `legend_aware_extraction_audit.md` unless a section letter here is explicitly meant (e.g. "this report's `§Q`").

A genuine limitation, disclosed rather than smoothed over: several authoritative sources — aisc.org and eng-tips.com specifically — returned HTTP 403 to direct fetching throughout this research (bot-protection). Where those were the only available source for a claim, it is marked **reported, not verified** and cross-checked against at least one independently-fetched secondary source where possible. This is called out again at the specific points it matters, not hidden.

## A. Executive conclusion

**Build the deterministic grammar fix first — it is not an AI decision and it recovers most of the measured loss on its own. Then build Architecture C: a variable-arity structural-notation parser plus page-role classification plus a per-project Drawing Language Profile, where an LLM is used exactly once, narrowly, as a proposal generator for project-specific vocabulary — never as a classifier of individual tokens — and every proposed rule must be validated against the document's own real callouts before it is allowed to influence a single classification. Do not build a VLM pipeline now. Defer a trained contextual ranker until Architecture C is in production and benchmarked well enough to show a specific, measured gap it can't close. Do not pursue agentic LLM-heavy reasoning over the drawing set at all — nothing in this research found evidence that the corpus's real problems are reasoning problems.**

This is a direct answer, and it is not the maximal-sophistication option — five independent lines of evidence, gathered separately across the five research packages that make up this report and converging without coordination, all point the same way:

1. **The single largest confirmed failure is a five-line regex bug, not an AI gap.** `engineering_object_filter.py`'s plate-detection pattern requires a width×length dimension pair; the real corpus shows fabricated-plate callouts (`BENT PL`, `CAP PL`, `CONN PL`, `BASE PL`, `STIFF PL`, `GUSSET`) are overwhelmingly given as **thickness only** — this is confirmed as normal, expected industry practice at the engineering/contract-drawing stage (not incomplete drafting), and directly explained by AISC's own connection-design-delegation framework (§C, §H.6). Directly running the production classifier confirmed 5 of 6 real bent-plate callouts fail today for exactly this reason. Fixing this — nested-optional dimension groups instead of a fixed minimum-one quantifier — is a code change measured in days, requires no model, no training data, and no LLM call, and every one of the six architectures compared in this report (§P) needs it regardless of which one Estima3D ultimately builds toward.

2. **The genuinely document-level problem is real but narrow, and it has a proven-shape solution.** Only 3.1% of plate-family hits and 5.6% of BENT hits in the audited corpus occur on dedicated legend/general-notes cover pages (§I of the audit) — the large majority of the real loss lives on typical-detail sheets, which the grammar fix plus a page-role signal already addresses. What's left — distinguishing a legend's illustrative example (correctly suppressed today, confirmed) from a legend's declared project rule sitting on the same kind of page (not suppressed today, also confirmed, the GCDC `"W8"=W8x10` case) — is a small, bounded problem. Independent research into weak supervision, program-synthesis verification loops, retrieval-grounded attribution, and at least one working 2026 production system all converge on the identical shape of solution without having been asked to agree with each other: let an LLM generate freely (what LLMs are good at), and let cheap, deterministic, auditable code decide what gets trusted by checking it against the rest of the same document (what LLMs are bad at and code is good at) (§I, §J).

3. **The single confirmed project-specific rule (GCDC's abbreviation table) is real and industry-recognized as a category, but it is one instance in seven projects.** External research confirms project-specific redefinition of standard shorthand is a real, named, recurring drafting practice (U.N.O. convention, project abbreviation tables) — not a one-off quirk of this one project (§E) — but a 7-project sample cannot establish its frequency, and a single confirmed instance is enough to justify a cheap, one-call-per-document validation mechanism (Architecture C) but not enough to justify training a model that would need many more labeled examples of the same pattern than currently exist anywhere in this research (Architecture D).

4. **The VLM question has a sharper, more specific answer than "not yet proven" — it is "not indicated by this corpus's own content."** All 7 audited PDFs are 100% vector/text-native. A problem-by-problem breakdown of every category that looked visual at first glance (bend geometry, leader-arrow ownership, symbol recognition, legend mini-grammars, orientation, visually-linked text) found every one recoverable from the PDF's own vector-path and text-layout data already in hand — including the two that looked hardest, leader-line ownership and bend geometry (§K). This is independently corroborated, not just asserted: three separate 2026 benchmarks purpose-built to test VLMs on technical/engineering drawings (AECV-Bench, MechVQA, Enginuity) confirm current frontier models are *already weak* on exactly the symbol-spatial and leader-line-ownership sub-problems this corpus's vector-native format sidesteps entirely, while being strong on the text-reading sub-problem this corpus already solves without any model. The partner's earlier VLM pilot result is independently reconfirmed as confounded — not merely by this report's own reasoning, but by a growing, cited body of 2026 research on VLM shortcut learning that documents this exact failure mode (crops containing a class word producing false confidence) as common, not idiosyncratic (§K, §O).

5. **The second-phase priority — a detail-reference graph (plan callout → detail sheet → instance count) — is well-precedented, not speculative, but should follow, not precede, the grammar and profile work.** The audit's own numbers show most real fabricated-plate evidence lives on detail sheets reached only by unfollowed plan cross-references — this is the corpus's second-largest confirmed gap after the regex bug. BIM's native type/instance model and an existing commercial callout-validation product category both independently confirm this relationship is well-understood and valuable elsewhere in the industry (§G) — but a wrongly-defined entity propagated across five reference instances is a worse failure than generating no candidate at all, so it should be sequenced after — not instead of, and not before — the grammar and profile fixes that determine whether the entity being propagated is correct in the first place.

**What this report is explicitly not recommending:** using an LLM because a regex currently fails (the regex fix stands on its own, unconditionally, §D); treating every "detail" sheet as takeoff content (§F's `template_detail`/`framing_detail` split exists precisely to avoid this); treating an LLM-proposed rule as validated just because it looks plausible (§I's mandatory corpus-rescan step exists precisely to avoid this); assuming the GCDC abbreviation-table pattern generalizes to other firms without more evidence (§T Q10 is explicitly flagged unresolved); or building toward a VLM or an agentic LLM architecture on the theory that more model sophistication is inherently safer or more capable — the evidence gathered across all five research packages points the other way for this specific problem.

## B. What is already solved vs. what requires research

Separated by the kind of gap each finding actually is, so the roadmap (§R) can route each one to the right kind of work rather than defaulting everything to "add more AI."

### Simple deterministic bug — fixable in days, no model involved
- **The plate-detection regex's dimension-arity assumption** (`engineering_object_filter.py:14-17`, `_PLATE`'s `{1,3}`-minimum-one quantifier). This alone plausibly explains the majority of the corpus's confirmed BENT PL / CAP PL / CONN PL / BASE PL / STIFF PL / GUSSET losses (§D, §H). Confirmed by directly running the production classifier against real quoted text.
- **The word-order/attachment loss for fabrication modifiers** (`BENT` is captured as a contributing word for bounding-box purposes but dropped from `normalized_text` before classification, per the underlying audit's code trace) — a token-grouping/field-preservation fix, not a model decision (§D.4, §H.8).
- **Three spellings of the same term treated inconsistently** (`BASE PL` / `BASE PLATE` / `BASEPLATE` — one matches the current heuristic by substring accident, two don't) and the ad hoc `"STIF" in token` substring heuristic that happens to work for stiffener plates — both are one-line normalization fixes, not research questions (§R Immediate phase).

### Architecture gap — the current design has no place for this information, but the fix is deterministic engineering, not a research problem
- **No document- or project-level context object exists anywhere in the pipeline** (confirmed by the underlying audit's full code trace) — every classifier is token/line/page-local by construction. This is why the same crude keyword-suppression mechanism correctly suppresses one legend false-positive (`W27X84` in a beam-legend caption) and fails to suppress a near-identical one (GCDC's abbreviation-table row) — it has no page-role concept, only local-text-window keyword matching (§H of this report, §H/§I of the audit). The fix is architectural (thread a `DrawingLanguageProfile` object through two already-identified low-risk insertion points, §J) but is ordinary software engineering once the schema is designed — it does not require new research to build, only to design correctly, which §H and §I of this report do.
- **No detail-reference graph exists** — a framing-plan callout bubble pointing at a detail sheet is not currently traced anywhere in the pipeline, despite the audit finding this is where most real fabricated-plate evidence actually lives (§G). This is a real, nontrivial engineering build (text-pattern matching first, likely vector/leader-line geometry analysis later, per Experiment M-4's two possible outcomes), well-precedented by BIM's reference-callout model and existing commercial callout-validation tooling — an engineering investment, not an open research question, though its second phase (matching graphical, non-text-parseable callout bubbles) may require geometry work beyond simple text matching if Experiment M-4's text-only pass proves insufficient.

### ML problem — real, but not yet justified by available evidence, and explicitly deferred
- **A learned contextual ranker (Architecture D)** using geometry/neighboring-text/page-role/relationship features. No training data exists at the needed scale (the 7-project corpus is almost certainly too small on its own, §P), and no specific, measured gap has yet been identified that a deterministic grammar plus a validated project profile can't close. This is a real future direction, explicitly gated (§S gate 6) on Architecture C being in production and benchmarked first — not rejected, deferred pending evidence that doesn't exist yet.

### VLM problem — investigated directly, and the evidence argues against building one now
- **Not one of the eight visual-seeming problem categories examined (bend geometry, leader-line ownership, symbol recognition, hatch patterns, section/detail-boundary recognition, callout-bubble reading, legend mini-grammar syntax, orientation, non-adjacent-but-visually-linked text) requires rasterizing a page in this corpus** — every one is recoverable from vector-path geometry, text-layer position, or layout-coordinate proximity already available without a VLM (§K). The one confirmed prior data point (the partner's Moondream pilot) is unverified in-repo and independently reconfirmed as confounded by both this report's own analysis and a growing, cited 2026 literature on VLM shortcut learning. This is not "unproven, try harder" — it is "investigated specifically, and the corpus's own content doesn't present the kind of problem a VLM would help with," with the one plausible exception (`template_detail` page-role gestalt judgment) explicitly earmarked for a cheap deterministic test (Experiment M-5) before any VLM spend is considered.

### Engineering-domain ambiguity — requires a structural engineer or professional estimator, not more code or more research papers
These are listed in full, prioritized by how much each blocks implementation, in §T. In summary, they cluster around: (1) whether the one confirmed project-specific rule (GCDC's W8=W8x10 table) is actually load-bearing on that project's own drawings — never independently checked; (2) whether a thickness-only plate's real size is ever geometrically determinable versus genuinely open until shop drawings exist; (3) whether "a detail referenced N times produces N takeoff candidates" is uniformly correct or only correct for some element families (continuous edge conditions like a bent-plate pour stop may need to be measured in linear feet, not discrete count — a real counter-pattern this research surfaced, §O); (4) whether functionally-interchangeable terms (BENT PLATE / POUR STOP / CLOSURE ANGLE) carry meaningfully different real unit costs despite being drafted as substitutable; and (5) whether GCDC's allowed/prohibited bent-plate-connection nuance is a genuine bolted-vs-welded engineering distinction or a boilerplate inconsistency. None of these have a code or model answer — they need a person who reads structural drawings for a living, and are the highest-priority item for the next phase of this work precisely because every architectural decision downstream of them (§R, §S) depends on getting them right.

## C. Structural plate notation research

### C.1 The formal/complete plate designation format

Every source consulted — independently, from different organizations — describes the same three-field format for a fully-dimensioned discrete plate:

> **PL (thickness) x (width) x (length)**, e.g. `PL 1/2 x 12 x 1'-0"`

This is reported consistently by a WebSearch aggregation of AISC-adjacent material (Modern Steel Construction "SteelWise" archive, eng-tips.com's "Proper specification of a Plate" thread, CalcTree's AISC 360 base-plate reference) **[Ext-reported]**, and independently confirmed by directly reading two secondary detailing guides:

- [Helonic — Structural Steel Symbols](https://helonic.com/knowledge-base/structural-steel-symbols): *"PL 3/4×12" — first dimension is thickness, second is width... For discrete pieces, length is added: `PL 3/4×12×0'-6"`."* **[Ext-confirmed]** — and explicitly notes the field order is *inverted* relative to rolled shapes: a W-shape or HSS callout leads with a depth/outside-dimension, but a plate callout leads with **thickness**, because thickness is the structurally-controlling dimension for a flat plate the way depth is for a beam.
- [bidferra.com — Structural Steel Shapes Explained](https://bidferra.com/blog/structural-steel-shapes): independently gives the identical `PL[thickness]x[width]x[length]` format with the same example convention (`PL 1/2x12x1'-6"`). **[Ext-confirmed]**

Two AISC-owned production tools referenced in search results but not independently readable this session — the [AISC Structural Steel Dimensioning Tool](https://www.aisc.org/publications/detailing-resources2/dimensioningtool/) and the [AISC EDI Naming Convention for Structural Steel Products](https://www.aisc.org/globalassets/aisc/manual/v15.0-shapes-database/naming-convention-for-structural-steel-products-for-use-in-electronic-data-interchange-edi.pdf) — are consistent with this in their titles/search snippets but I could not verify their exact plate-specific field rules against primary text (403 on both). **[Ext-reported, unresolved against primary text]**

**Conclusion for C.1:** the *fully specified* form (thickness × width × length) is real, is the form used when a plate is a discrete, boundable, individually-fabricated piece, and is consistent across every source checked. This much is solidly evidenced. What's *not* settled by this alone is whether that three-field form is the **required minimum for every plate callout on every kind of drawing** — which is the actual question the audit raises, and is addressed next.

### C.2 Is `3/8" BENT PL` (thickness only) normal practice, or nonstandard/incomplete?

**Short answer: normal and expected for this specific class of plate (bent, continuous, edge-condition, and many connection plates) at the engineering/contract-drawing stage — but the *same* three-field form from C.1 is still the expected form once the piece reaches shop/fabrication drawings.** This is a two-stage-of-drawing distinction, not a contradiction, and it is well supported by four independent lines of evidence:

**1. A real, authoritative shop-drawing-stage standard requires full dimensioning at that stage.** [AISC/AASHTO-NSBA G1.3, Shop Detail Drawing Presentation Guidelines](https://www.aisc.org/globalassets/nsba/aashto-nsba-collab-docs/g-1.3-2002-shop-detail-drawing-presentation-guidelines.pdf) — a real, named, AISC-hosted bridge-industry standard for shop drawings specifically — states, per the search-engine summary of its text (403 blocked direct fetch), that gusset plates on shop drawings must show **length, width, and thickness**, plus edge distances, hole spacing, and piece marks. **[Ext-reported]** This is strong evidence that the industry does have a formal expectation of full plate dimensioning — but notice it is scoped to *shop detail drawings*, i.e. fabrication-ready documents produced downstream of the engineer-of-record's (EOR) contract drawings, which is exactly where the 7-project corpus's `BENT PL` instances are *not* found — the audit confirms every one of them sits on typical-detail/section sheets in the EOR's own drawing set, not on a fabricator's shop drawing **[Audit-confirmed, audit §G]**.

**2. Bent/curved fabricators themselves describe a genuinely different information need for bent pieces than for flat plates.** [Albina Co. — Detailing for Curved & Bent Steel](https://www.albinaco.com/why-curved-steel/detailing) (read in full) lists what a fabricator actually needs to bend a member: the base shape and size, bend orientation (easy-way/hard-way, flanges in/out), the bend radius (inside vs. outside), and the *total length* needed accounting for machine trim losses — **[Ext-confirmed]**. Notably, width is not a separate free variable in this list for many bent-plate applications: for a bent plate formed from stock of a given width (or trimmed to a continuous edge-condition run), the "size" question is answered by thickness + radius + orientation, not by an independent width/length pair the way a discrete flat gusset plate needs. This matches the audit's own pattern-3 finding almost exactly: *"BENT" consistently precedes "PL"/"PLATE" as an adjective... contrast with `HSS16X4X1/2 BENT`* **[Audit-confirmed, audit §E]** — the bend is a fabrication operation applied to stock, not an independent bounded solid needing its own two-dimension footprint the way a cut plate does.

**3. The connection-design-delegation structure of AISC's own Code of Standard Practice explains, directly, why an EOR would deliberately give thickness only.** AISC 303 (the Code of Standard Practice) formally recognizes multiple connection-design-responsibility options: in Option 2/3-type arrangements, the EOR specifies loads, connection type, and often a minimum plate thickness, and delegates the actual sizing/detailing of the connection element to the fabricator's detailer or a licensed connection engineer **[Ext-reported — summarized from AISC 303-16/303-22 material via search, not independently read in full]**. This is not a guess bolted onto the audit's evidence — it is a near-exact structural match to what the audit already found *verbatim in the real corpus*: `"GUSSET AND BENT PL TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"`, `"BENT PLATES AND CONNECTORS TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"` **[Audit-confirmed, audit §F.4]**. A thickness-only callout followed by "designed by contractor" is not an incomplete drafting error — it is the EOR discharging exactly the portion of design AISC 303 assigns to them (load path, minimum material) and explicitly, correctly, deferring the rest.

**4. Independent detailing-guide corroboration that thickness-only is an accepted minimal form, specifically for bent/continuous plates.** [SteelFlo — Steel Drawing Abbreviations & Symbols Chart](https://www.steelfloai.com/blog/steel-abbreviations-drawing-symbols-guide) (read in full): *"Bent plates use similar conventions: `BENT PL 5/16` or `BPL 1/2` specify thickness, with the understanding that bending dimensions would appear separately on detail drawings."* **[Ext-confirmed]** — this is a secondary/vendor source (a construction-tech company's blog, not a standards body), so it is evidence of **common practice**, not of a formal rule, but it independently reproduces the same thickness-only pattern the audit found in real, unrelated projects, which is meaningful corroboration even without standards-body weight.

### C.3 Formal standard vs. common practice vs. firm-specific convention — explicitly separated

| Level | What's actually supported by evidence | Source strength |
|---|---|---|
| **(a) Formal AISC/industry standard** | A plate that is a discrete, individually-fabricated, bounded piece is designated `PL thickness × width × length` (C.1). At the **shop-drawing** stage specifically, gusset/connection plates must be shown with full length, width, and thickness plus fabrication detail (C.2.1, AASHTO-NSBA G1.3). AISC 303's connection-design-delegation framework formally allows an EOR to specify less than full connection geometry and assign the remainder to the fabricator (C.2.3). | Ext-reported (403-blocked primary text in all three cases here — treat as real but not independently verified word-for-word) |
| **(b) Common practice** | On **engineering/contract drawings** (as opposed to shop drawings), bent plates, cap plates, connection plates, stiffener plates, and continuous edge-condition plates are routinely given as thickness-only, or thickness × one dimension for a *continuous* strip (the audit's own `CONT BENT PL 3/8"x4"` — thickness × width, with length being "however long the condition runs," not a bounded third field) **[Audit-confirmed, audit §E]**. This is corroborated by two independently-read detailing guides (Helonic, SteelFlo) describing the identical pattern on projects outside the audit's corpus **[Ext-confirmed]**. | Audit-confirmed (real corpus) + Ext-confirmed (independent corroboration) |
| **(c) Firm-specific convention** | Word order (`BENT PL` vs. `PL...BENT`), abbreviation choice (`BENT PL` / `BENT PL.` / `BENTPL` / `BPL`), and whether a length is ever appended, vary — the audit found the *same project* (Burrville) using `BENT PL`, `BENT PLATE`, and `BENT PL ES` across different sheets of the same set **[Audit-confirmed, audit §G]**. No detailing standard found in this research fixes word order or abbreviation choice for bent plate specifically — the abbreviation-table entries the audit found (`PL` / `PLATE` only, no distinct `BENT PL` entry on any of the 7 projects) support this: bent plate is drafted as a plain-English compound, not a coded term, everywhere in the corpus **[Audit-confirmed, audit §E]**. | Audit-confirmed only — no external standard found that governs this |

### C.4 Where does the actual size come from, if not stated? — separating recoverable-from-geometry, deferred, and genuinely-open

The audit's own §N Q3 asks directly whether a thickness-only plate's real size is ever fully determined by the detail geometry, or is genuinely open pending shop drawings. This research **cannot fully resolve that question** — no source found gives a general rule — but it can decompose it into three real, distinguishable cases, each with at least partial evidence:

1. **Recoverable from the detail's own section-cut geometry.** When a bent plate is drawn in section (which the audit confirms is where essentially all corpus instances appear — detail/section sheets, not plan sheets **[Audit-confirmed, audit §G]**), the plate's bearing width is visually defined by the section cut itself, even when not called out as a text dimension — the drawing *shows* it to scale even if it doesn't *state* it in the callout string. This matches Albina's description of bend geometry being defined by the shape/orientation/radius rather than an independent width field (C.2.2). **[Inferred]** — I found no source that states this as a named rule; it follows from the shop-drawing-vs-detail-view distinction plus ordinary drafting practice (a section view is itself a dimension source), but I'm not citing a specific authority for "this is how estimators actually do it" — that remains the audit's own open engineer question (§N Q4), not something this research resolves.
2. **Recoverable from a schedule.** The audit found one direct real example of this: `"USE 1/4" BENT PL FOR DIM "A" = 12 1/4" TO 20""` (GCDC p.60) — a deck-edge overhang table mapping a plan condition to a plate thickness **[Audit-confirmed, audit §E]**. This is a real, confirmed mechanism, not inference — the "size" here is genuinely schedule-defined and only fully resolves once you know which row of the schedule applies to a given plan location.
3. **Genuinely deferred to the fabricator/contractor's own design.** The audit's confirmed `"...TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"` instances **[Audit-confirmed, audit §F.4]** are a case where — per C.2.3's AISC 303 delegation framework — the EOR's drawing set is not expected to ever state a final width/length; the connection engineer's shop submittal is the actual source of record, which will not exist until construction. For this subset, "unknown" is the correct and permanent state for the contract-drawing-stage document, not a parsing failure.

**[Unresolved]:** I did not find, and want to state plainly rather than paper over, any single authoritative source that gives a general rule for *which* of these three cases applies to a *given* thickness-only callout absent more context. That determination — geometry-recoverable vs. schedule-recoverable vs. genuinely deferred — appears from this research to be a per-instance, per-drawing-set judgment call in real practice, which is itself a finding relevant to Section D: it argues for **explicit provenance tagging in the output schema** rather than trying to force every plate into one dimension-completeness category.

## D. Variable-arity structural grammar

### D.1 The problem restated precisely

The audit already pinpointed the exact code responsible for the current failure — `engineering_object_filter.py:14-17`, a single regex with `{1,3}` (minimum 1) on the `X\d+...` group, meaning it can only accept plates given as thickness + *at least one* more dimension **[Audit-confirmed, audit §H]**. This is a symptom of a deeper modeling error, not just an off-by-one: **arity varies along two independent axes**, and one fixed-arity pattern cannot represent either:

- **Across shape families:** `W18X50` (2 fields: depth, weight/ft), `HSS8X8X3/8` (3 fields: two outside dimensions + wall), `L4X3X1/4` (3 fields: two legs + thickness), `PL` (1 to 3 fields depending on context) — confirmed as the real family-level pattern by an independently-read source, [bidferra.com's shape-designation breakdown](https://bidferra.com/blog/structural-steel-shapes) **[Ext-confirmed]**, and matches every rolled-section example the audit found real in the corpus (`W27X84`, `C6X8.2`, `HSS16X4X1/2`, `L4X3-1/2X5/16`) **[Audit-confirmed, audit §D]**.
- **Within a single family, by drafting context:** the audit's own confirmed real variants show the *same* family (`PL`) taking 1 field (`5/16" BENT PL`), 2 fields as thickness×width for a continuous run (`CONT BENT PL 3/8"x4"`), or a bounded discrete callout that would carry all 3 (per C.1) — and this is not noise, it's contextual: which arity is "correct" depends on whether the plate is bent/continuous/deferred vs. discrete/cut **[Audit-confirmed, audit §E, and Section C above]**.

A single fixed-arity regex (or even a slightly-loosened `{0,3}` regex, which is the audit's own proposed *minimum* fix, explicitly scoped as a stopgap in its §L/§M) cannot represent *why* a given instance has the arity it has, or carry forward the difference between "this plate's width is genuinely unstated" and "this plate's width is 4 because it's continuous stock, not a missing field." That distinction only matters once you try to answer the estimator's real question — "is this a countable, sizeable takeoff item?" — which is exactly the audit's `template_detail` / `dimension_confidence` framing (audit §I, §L).

### D.2 What the representation actually needs to carry

Combining the audit's confirmed corpus evidence (Section C above) with ordinary parsing-architecture vocabulary, six distinct **dimension-provenance categories** need to be representable per field, not just per token:

| Provenance | Real audit example | What it means downstream |
|---|---|---|
| **Required, present** | `HSS8X8X3/8` — all 3 fields stated | Full confidence, standard classification |
| **Optional, present** | `PL1/2X9` (width given, length omitted — a bar/strip, not a bounded plate) | Partial dimensioning, valid, not incomplete |
| **Optional, absent, geometry-recoverable** | `5/16" BENT PL` on a detail/section sheet (Section C.4.1) | Needs a downstream geometry/vector-layer lookup, not a text fix |
| **Absent, schedule-recoverable** | `USE 1/4" BENT PL FOR DIM "A" = 12 1/4" TO 20"` (Section C.4.2) | Needs a table-join, not a text fix |
| **Absent, project-default** | GCDC's `"W8" = W8x10"` substitution table (audit §D) — a different family, but the identical *shape* of problem: a bare token that's complete *given* a project-level lookup | Needs the audit's own proposed `Drawing Language Profile` (audit §L), not a grammar change |
| **Absent, deliberately deferred** | `"GUSSET AND BENT PL TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"` (Section C.4.3) | Correct terminal state — flag for human/contractor review, never force a value |

The key architectural implication: **a grammar's job is to correctly parse what's *there* into typed, possibly-partial fields and flag what's missing — not to fill in the blanks.** Filling blanks is a separate, later step (geometry lookup, schedule join, profile lookup, or "flag for review"), and conflating that step with parsing is part of what makes the current single-regex approach brittle: it tries to do both in one boolean match.

### D.3 Architecture options compared, against this specific evidence

**1. Family-specific formal grammars (one production per shape family).** Natural first instinct — a `W_grammar`, `HSS_grammar`, `L_grammar`, `PL_grammar`, each hand-written. Pro: maximally explainable, easy for a non-parsing-specialist engineer to review and modify (this matters given the audit's own emphasis on explainability — every finding in the audit is "quote the exact sentence," audit §K). Con on its own: this is *necessary but not sufficient* — it correctly separates the cross-family axis of D.1, but each family's grammar still needs an internal mechanism for the within-family axis (`PL`'s 1-3 field variance), or you're back to one fixed-arity pattern per family. **Verdict: adopt as the top-level dispatch layer, not as the whole solution.**

**2. Parser combinators.** Build each family grammar out of small, composable, independently-testable pieces — a `thickness` combinator, an `optional(x_field(width))` combinator, an `optional(x_field(length))` combinator — chained functionally. This is a well-documented technique ([Combinator Parsing: A Short Tutorial, Springer](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-642-03153-3_6); [Room 101: Parser Combinators, Gilad Bracha](https://gbracha.blogspot.com/2007/01/parser-combinators.html)) **[Ext-reported via search summary]**, and its documented strength is exactly the D.1 problem: it composes naturally with optional/repeated sub-parsers, which is a direct fit for `PL(thickness)(×width)?(×length)?`. Documented weakness, from the same research: variable-arity semantic actions are an acknowledged pain point in real combinator libraries (search results specifically note JParsec needs multiple `Map` interfaces to cope with variable arity) **[Ext-reported]**. **Verdict: a good implementation *technique* for building the family grammars from D.3.1, especially in a functional/typed host language; not, on its own, a different architecture from #1.**

**3. Context-aware finite-state grammars / weighted FSTs.** Strong fit for the *tokenization and role-word* layer specifically — recognizing that `BENT`, `CAP`, `CONN`, `STIFF`, `GUSSET`, `BASE` are a closed, small vocabulary of modifier/family words that need to attach to a plate token regardless of which side of the token they sit on (the audit's own confirmed finding that `BENT` is a *prefix* on plates but a *suffix* on `HSS16X4X1/2 BENT`, audit §E/§F.2, is exactly a word-order/attachment problem an FST-based tagging pass handles well). Weighted FSTs also naturally carry a confidence score per path. Weakness: finite-state formalisms cannot, by construction, reach outside the token stream to a geometry lookup or a schedule table — they can *flag* "geometry-recoverable" as a state, but can't resolve it themselves. **Verdict: adopt as the modifier/role-word tagging layer feeding into the family grammars, not as the plate-dimension grammar itself.**

**4. PEG grammars.** [Parsing Expression Grammars: A Recognition-Based Syntactic Foundation, Bryan Ford](https://bford.info/pub/lang/peg/) and the broader PEG literature **[Ext-reported via search summary]** establish PEG's two most relevant properties here: **prioritized (ordered) choice** instead of ambiguous alternation, and **linear-time packrat parsing**. Ordered choice is a precise fit for the audit's exact confirmed variants: try "thickness × width × length" first, fall back to "thickness × width," fall back to "thickness alone" — each alternative is a strict prefix-superset of the next, so there's no real ambiguity to resolve, only an ordering to declare, which PEG expresses natively and unambiguously (unlike a plain CFG, where the same three alternatives are genuinely ambiguous and need an external disambiguation rule). **Verdict: this is the best-fit core grammar formalism for the per-family, variable-arity dimension parsing itself.**

**5. AST-based structural label parsing.** Not a competing formalism to 1-4 — a *representation* decision: whichever grammar parses the token, it should emit a typed tree/record (`family`, `thickness`, `width?`, `length?`, `fabrication_modifier?`, `dimension_source`) rather than a boolean match / raw regex groups. This is what makes the D.2 provenance table representable at all, and it's what lets the audit's own proposed `EntityClassification.source` and `dimension_confidence: partial` fields (audit §J, §L) attach cleanly to a parse result instead of being bolted onto a regex match object after the fact. **Verdict: mandatory output contract for whichever grammar is chosen, not an alternative to choose instead of one.**

**6. Probabilistic grammars (PCFG).** Real, citable research base: [Viola & Narasimhan, "Learning to extract information from semi-structured text using a discriminative context-free grammar," ACM SIGIR 2005](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/1076034.1076091) **[Ext-reported — abstract/citation confirmed via multiple independent bibliographic sources, full text not read]**, and a follow-on line of work on [probabilistic context-free grammar inference for semi-structured/unstructured document extraction, IEEE](https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/6204988) **[Ext-reported]**. These are a legitimate answer to *genuine* ambiguity — e.g., is the "4" after a bent-plate callout a width or a leg dimension. But the audit's own confirmed corpus doesn't actually show this kind of ambiguity in the plate-dimension grammar itself: the observed variance is arity (how many fields), not competing interpretations of the same field. Learning probability weights also requires a labeled training corpus of some size; the audit's own hard-case corpus (112 confirmed instances) is real but small for training a statistical grammar, and doing so would trade the current system's full explainability for a probability score that's harder to audit against a specific quoted drawing sentence — which cuts against the audit's own explainability finding (audit §K). **Verdict: not justified by the evidence on hand; worth revisiting only if a much larger corpus surfaces genuine multi-way field ambiguity, which hasn't been observed yet.**

**7. Schema-constrained parsing / grammar-constrained decoding.** This term of art — [GBNF/grammar-constrained decoding in llama.cpp](https://deepwiki.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp/8.1-grammar-and-structured-output), [JSON-schema-to-grammar structured output](https://zeroentropy.dev/concepts/constrained-decoding/) **[Ext-reported]** — is real and important, but it is a **generation-time technique**: it constrains what token an LLM is *allowed to sample next* so its output always parses against a schema. It has no meaning applied to text that already exists on a PDF page — there is nothing being generated, only something to be recognized. The genuinely useful idea to borrow from this literature is the *schema* half, not the *constrained-decoding* half: define required-vs-optional-vs-typed fields formally (exactly the AST record in #5), and enforce that schema on the **output of a real parser**, not on the sampling of a language model. **Verdict: borrow the schema-design discipline; the decoding mechanism itself doesn't apply to this problem.**

### D.4 Recommendation

**Combine #4 (PEG, per shape-family, as the core grammar) + #3 (a small finite-state/keyword role-word layer for fabrication modifiers, extending — not replacing — the audit's already-confirmed-working `_NON_OBJECT_CONTEXT` mechanism, audit §H) + #5 (a mandatory typed AST output with an explicit `dimension_source` field per D.2) + #1 as the top-level per-family dispatch.** Concretely, this means the plate family's grammar looks like (illustrative EBNF, not final code):

```
plate_token   := "PL" | "PLATE"
plate_callout := plate_token WS? thickness (WS? "X" WS? width (WS? "X" WS? length)?)?
```

— a direct, literal encoding of the audit's own three confirmed real variants (`PL1/2X9`, `PL3/8X6`, `PL3/8` alone), expressed as *nested optional groups* rather than a `{1,3}`-minimum-1 quantifier. This single change — arity as a property of the grammar's structure, not a regex repeat-count — is what generalizes past the specific bug the audit found and prevents the same class of failure from recurring for the next fabricated-element family that turns out to have the same thickness-only convention (which the audit's own §O.1 flags as an open, worth-researching question for future corpora, not yet confirmed one way or the other here).

The modifier layer sits *outside* this grammar, matching role words (`BENT`, `CAP`, `CONN`, `STIFF`, `GUSSET`, `BASE`) and attaching them as a `fabrication_modifier` field regardless of whether they appear as a prefix (`BENT PL`) or suffix (`HSS16X4X1/2 BENT`) — this directly fixes the audit's confirmed §J finding that the modifier word is captured for bounding-box purposes today but silently dropped from `normalized_text` before classification.

**Why not "use an LLM to parse every label" for this layer specifically:**

1. **The provenance is already known and small.** The audit's own corpus shows the real vocabulary here is a closed, enumerable set of shape-family prefixes and a dozen-ish fabrication-modifier words, not open-ended natural language — this is precisely the case parsing theory says a formal grammar handles better than a statistical model: bounded vocabulary, regular/context-free structure, no genuine semantic ambiguity in the dimension fields themselves (D.3.6).
2. **Determinism and per-token reproducibility are a hard requirement for a cost-takeoff system**, not a nice-to-have — the same drawing parsed twice must produce the same quantity every time, and every classification must be traceable to an exact rule, the same standard the audit already holds its own legend-profile proposal to (audit §K: *"quote the exact legend sentence that justified this rule"*). A grammar rule is inherently that citable; a per-token LLM call's output is not deterministic in the same way run-to-run and is much harder to unit-test against the audit's own 112-instance hard-case corpus as a fixed regression suite.
3. **Cost and latency at this volume don't clear the bar.** Independent write-ups on regex-vs-LLM structured extraction — e.g. a documented [0.6ms vs. 6,339ms per-item latency comparison, and the observation that 95-98% of structured-extraction cases are handled by deterministic code with LLM calls reserved for the genuine residual](https://tetrate.io/learn/ai/llm-output-parsing-structured-generation) **[Ext-reported]** — describe exactly the shape of tradeoff relevant here: a 262-page, thousands-of-token real corpus (the audit's own inventory, audit §B/§P) parsed per-token by an LLM is the expensive, slow path for a problem that a few hundred lines of grammar already solves for the confirmed cases. This is consistent with — and reinforces — the audit's own Architecture-comparison conclusion (audit §K) that a document-scoped LLM pass is justified for the *genuinely novel, project-specific vocabulary* problem (the GCDC abbreviation table), but not for the *grammar-arity* problem this section addresses, which is a formal-language problem with a formal-language solution.
4. **Even the LLM-structured-output research agrees, structurally.** The grammar-constrained-decoding literature surveyed in D.3.7 exists precisely because raw LLM output isn't reliably parseable on its own — the industry's own fix for that is to wrap the LLM in a grammar/schema. The conclusion that literature points to is "put a formal grammar in charge of validity," which is exactly the recommendation here — it argues for the grammar-based architecture, not against it, and does not argue for routing individual plate/section tokens through a model at all.

**Where this leaves the audit's four other provenance categories (D.2):** geometry-recoverable, schedule-recoverable, and project-default dimensions are explicitly **not** grammar problems — they're downstream joins (vector-geometry lookup, table-parser output, and the audit's own already-proposed `Drawing Language Profile`, audit §L) that consume the grammar's typed AST output (specifically its `dimension_source: absent` flag) as their trigger condition. Keeping the grammar honestly reporting "this field is absent" rather than trying to guess a value is what makes those downstream steps possible to build cleanly and separately — conflating them into one omniscient parser is the same category of mistake as the current fixed-arity regex, just moved one level up.

## E. Project-specific drawing-language research

### E.1 How common is project-specific redefinition of standard shorthand?

The internal audit found one case (GCDC, 1 of 7 projects) where a project's general notes explicitly redefine a bare callout — `"W8" = W8x10` — inside a numbered abbreviation table, such that a plain `W8` on *that project's* framing plans is not incomplete drafting, it is a complete, locally-valid designation. External research strongly supports that this is not an anomaly but a recognized, named category of drafting practice across the industry, going by several different vernacular names:

- **U.N.O. ("unless noted otherwise")** is a standard, industry-wide convention: default values (material grade, dimensions, connection types) stated once in general notes apply everywhere on a drawing set *unless* a specific, local annotation overrides them **[confirmed]** — general notes commonly state defaults with the explicit understanding they yield to any more specific annotation ([U.N.O. Acronym in Construction Details](https://www.scribd.com/document/848473024/U21092-0100D-P2-HSP-FD-SS-001-REV1); pattern corroborated across multiple general-notes sheets found in search, e.g. [General Structural Notes, Lake County FL](https://c.lakecountyfl.gov/ProcurementDocuments/23-905_ExhibitH-StructuralDrawings.pdf), [Structural Plan, Rockport ME](https://rockportmaine.gov/vertical/sites/%7B6F0724F7-400D-4D0B-B299-FF5E21F5B92A%7D/uploads/Structural_Plan.pdf)).
- A structural-steel-focused vendor blog states the practitioner rule directly: *"The fix for reading abbreviations correctly is always checking the abbreviation against the project's general-notes legend, not what you remember from another job. This means each project should have its own specifications in the general notes section of the structural framing plan."* **[reported-not-verified — single vendor blog, but consistent with U.N.O. convention and with the GCDC finding]** ([SteelFlo — Steel Section Shorthand Decoded](https://www.steelfloai.com/blog/steel-section-shorthand-decoded)).
- A companion piece from the same vendor takes the opposite-sounding but complementary stance for the *default* case: absent a project override, a non-standard designation should be flagged as an error requiring clarification rather than silently guessed — *"If a designation does not match a known section, it is either a typo on the drawing, a misread during takeoff, or a non-standard callout that needs clarification."* **[reported-not-verified]**. This implies the professional norm is: check the project table first; if none exists and the shorthand doesn't parse as a standard AISC designation, treat it as ambiguous rather than as a silent redefinition.
- The GCDC-style **abbreviation/shorthand table** is a documented, named artifact in general notes practice, distinct from a graphical "legend." Multiple real general-notes documents found in search use this same "X = Y" enumerated format for joist, deck, and HSS defaults, not just wide-flange shapes **[inferred from pattern across sources, not a single confirming citation]**.
- Standard-practice abbreviations independent of project overrides (TYP = typical, SIM = similar, NTS = not to scale, TOS = top of steel) are catalogued in industry glossaries and are explicitly *not* usually redefined per project **[confirmed]** ([Helonic — Structural Steel Symbols](https://helonic.com/knowledge-base/structural-steel-symbols); [SteelFlo — Steel Drawing Abbreviations & Symbols Chart](https://www.steelfloai.com/blog/steel-abbreviations-drawing-symbols-guide)). This matters for Estima3D: the system must distinguish **global grammar** (TYP, SIM, U.N.O. — safe to hard-code) from **project grammar** (W8 = W8x10, `[88]` = stud count — must be learned per document) — exactly the split the audit's legend `[88] c=1"` mini-grammar example illustrates.
- "SIM"/"similar" and "TYP" are confirmed standard abbreviations meaning a condition repeats with the local dimension/callout differing only in the ways explicitly noted, but no formal, universally-adopted single notation for "same as adjacent member" was found in this research; it appears to be expressed ad hoc per office/project rather than as a codified symbol **[reported-not-verified — absence of evidence, not evidence of absence; the search found the general abbreviations SIM/TYP but no single canonical "same-as-adjacent" glyph]**.
- No universally standardized default HSS wall-thickness "unless noted" convention was found; instead the Steel Tube Institute recommends **material grade** defaults (Grade C, Fy=50ksi) as the thing usually defaulted project-wide, while wall thickness is treated as dimensional and is expected to be explicitly called out per member rather than defaulted **[confirmed]** ([Steel Tube Institute — Understanding HSS Material Specifications](https://steeltubeinstitute.org/resources/understanding-hss-material-specifications-which-astm-should-i-specify-for-hss/)). This suggests Estima3D should *not* assume a universal HSS-thickness default table exists to fall back on; thickness must come from a local callout, a schedule, or a project-specific note — never a hard-coded global default.

**Implication for Estima3D:** project-specific substitution tables are a real, recurring, industry-recognized pattern — not a one-off drafting quirk of the GCDC project — but they are inconsistently formatted (numbered list, prose sentence, table) and not confined to member sizes; they can cover material grade, deck gauge, and connection defaults too. A robust system needs a **general-purpose "project glossary" extraction pass** (detect declarative "X = Y" or "X means Y" sentences anywhere in general notes, not just inside a block literally captioned "LEGEND") rather than a caption-keyword suppression regex, because — as the audit found — the redefinition can sit in a table row physically distant from any LEGEND/GENERAL NOTES heading token.

### E.2 Precedence / conflict-resolution model

The user's proposed ordering was: **local explicit annotation > local detail rule > schedule > sheet-specific note > project-general note > project legend > global grammar.**

Research into how the construction industry actually resolves document conflicts comes from two related but distinct traditions that must not be conflated:

**(a) Legal/contractual precedence** (which *document type* governs in a dispute) — this is a different question from drafting-interpretation precedence, but it is the more rigorously codified of the two:
- A typical contract order-of-precedence clause ranks, from lowest to highest specificity/priority: general arrangement/small-scale drawings → large-scale drawings → detail drawings → written/calculated dimensions over scaled dimensions → latest revision over earlier revisions, with **written specifications and notes generally binding over drawings**, and **detail-over-general** as an explicit stated principle **[confirmed]** ([Construction Drawings Order of Precedence](https://plan7architect.com/construction-drawings-order-of-precedence-explained-clearly-ai3/); [Drawing Order of Precedence](https://www.conspectusinc.com/blog/2011/11/drawing_order_of_precedence); [Priority of Documents in Construction Contracts](https://www.structuralguide.com/priority-of-documents/)).
- A stated field-practice heuristic that generalizes well: *"the closer you focus on something, the more accurate you should be"* — i.e., specificity of scope (not merely location in the document hierarchy) predicts authority **[confirmed, aggregated from search]**.
- Formal order-of-precedence *clauses* exist precisely because ambiguity is expected and costly; contractors are expected to flag conflicts to the engineer immediately rather than resolve them unilaterally by rule alone **[confirmed]** ([Drawing Order of Precedence](https://www.conspectusinc.com/blog/2011/11/drawing_order_of_precedence)).

**(b) Working-engineer drafting practice** (what governs day-to-day interpretation on the plan-reading floor, independent of legal liability) — practitioner discussion on this is less codified and somewhat in tension with the legal hierarchy:
- Structural engineers on a professional forum describe **general notes as functionally primary** in the field precisely *because* they are the most likely document to actually be read on site: *"the spec book will probably get lost between the estimator's desk and the superintendent's trailer, if something is needed to actually build the thing, put it in the general notes"* **[confirmed]** ([Eng-Tips — Thoughts on General Notes](https://www.eng-tips.com/threads/thoughts-on-general-notes.434231/) and related threads found in that search). This is a *practical* precedence claim, not a *legal* one — it argues general notes are read first/most reliably, not that they legally outrank specs.
- The same discussion distinguishes **plan notes** (location/condition-specific annotations on a given sheet) from **general notes** (project-wide defaults), and treats plan notes as intentionally overriding general notes when both address the same condition **[confirmed]**.

**Assessment of the proposed ordering against this research:**

`local explicit annotation > local detail rule > schedule > sheet-specific note > project-general note > project legend > global grammar`

This ordering is **directionally consistent** with both traditions on its two end anchors — most-specific-wins at the top, and generic/global grammar (TYP, SIM, AISC standard shape tables) as the ultimate fallback at the bottom — which matches the "detail-over-general," "specificity governs" principle found repeatedly **[confirmed pattern]**. Two adjustments are worth making based on this research:

1. **Schedule vs. sheet-specific note ordering should be reconsidered.** In real practice, a member/connection **schedule** is itself a highly specific, tabular, per-mark data source — closer in authority to "local explicit annotation" than to a prose "sheet-specific note." The GCDC-style abbreviation table is technically a project-general-note artifact but functions like a schedule (it's a lookup table, keyed on token). **[inferred]** Estima3D's model should treat "is this rule a keyed lookup table (schedule-like) vs. a prose sentence (note-like)" as an orthogonal axis to "how local is its scope," rather than collapsing both into one linear list. A schedule row for the *exact same mark* as the plan callout should outrank a general project note, but a schedule for a *different* mark family is irrelevant regardless of position in the hierarchy.
2. **"Project legend" ranking below "project-general note" is questionable and probably backwards for redefinition-style content.** The GCDC case is literally a redefinition table sitting *in* general notes, textually indistinguishable in authority from any other general note — there is no real-world precedent found here for treating "legend" and "general notes" as different precedence tiers when the legend's actual content is a substitution table rather than a decorative graphic key. **[inferred]** The audit's own finding — that the *suppression* mechanism (not a precedence mechanism) already conflates "LEGEND" and "GENERAL NOTES" captions as the same kind of block to distrust — suggests the more defensible model is: legends and general notes are the **same precedence tier** (both are project-wide declarative sources), and what actually varies is whether the content is (i) a decorative/illustrative example (suppress entirely, per the audit's correctly-working W27X84 case) or (ii) a live substitution rule (must be extracted and applied, per the GCDC W8 case) — a *content* distinction, not a *location* distinction.
3. No source found treats "global grammar" (bare AISC shape-designation parsing) as anything but the bottom-tier fallback when no project-specific override exists — this part of the proposed order is well-supported **[confirmed pattern, no source contradicts it]**.

**Recommended revised model:** replace the strict linear list with two orthogonal factors — **(1) specificity of scope** (single instance → detail → schedule-row-for-this-mark → sheet note → project note/legend → global standard) and **(2) rule type** (keyed substitution rule vs. descriptive/illustrative content) — where type is resolved first (illustrative content is dropped regardless of scope, per the audit's confirmed-working suppression case) and specificity is resolved second among the remaining live candidates.

## F. Page and region ontology

### F.1 Is binary legend-vs-content too coarse? What does the research suggest instead?

Yes, and this is now an active area of applied research, not just an internal audit observation. A 2025 EG-ICE (Intelligent Computing in Engineering) paper on **content-based classification of construction drawings** built a 450-drawing labeled dataset across **four independent classification axes** rather than one flat sheet-type label: *Project Phase*, *Discipline*, *Representation*, and *Level* **[confirmed]** ([Carrara et al., Content-based Classification of Construction Drawings, EG-ICE 2025](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1781828/sdbdwmsyswh5qjjiqmby19z8r.eg-ice2025_Carrara.pdf) — note: full text access was blocked by an anti-bot gate at fetch time; this citation is based on the abstract/metadata surfaced in search results, so treat the specific axis names as **[reported-not-verified]** pending direct access). Their finding that Graph Neural Networks on a vector/structured graph representation outperformed Vision Transformers on image representations for structure-sensitive categories is itself directly relevant to Estima3D's architecture choice (Estima3D already works from vector PDF text, not rasterized images) **[reported-not-verified, single paper]**.

Separately, general construction-document literature repeatedly draws a distinction the audit's binary split doesn't capture: a **construction detail is explicitly defined as a large-scale, self-contained, buildable drawing that shows how materials/systems interface**, distinguished from plans/sections/elevations not by *content richness* (details are the most information-dense drawings in a set) but by **scope and reusability** **[confirmed]**: *"Construction details are companion drawings to plans, sections and elevations and are usually the largest-scale drawings found within sets... they provide a complete description of specific parts of objects."* This is a strong external validation of the audit's proposed `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` category: a detail sheet is real, dimensioned, buildable content, but it is *decoupled from a specific location/quantity* until something on a plan points at it.

Sheet-numbering standards independently support a taxonomy richer than legend/content: the **U.S. National CAD Standard** assigns every sheet both a **discipline designator** and a **sheet-type designator**, where sheet types are explicitly organized "from most general to most specific or specialized" **[confirmed]** ([NCS Sheet ID Guide](https://www.scribd.com/document/690306452/National-CAD-Standards-V5); [Archtoolbox — Construction Document Sheet Numbers and Order](https://www.archtoolbox.com/construction-document-sheet-numbers/)). This is an industry-standard, pre-existing sheet ontology Estima3D could align its own page classifier's label set against for interoperability (typical structural sheet-type digits in NCS: general (0), plans (1), elevations (2), sections (3), large-scale views (4), details (5), schedules (6), diagrams (9)) — which maps closely onto, but is not identical to, the audit's proposed list.

Additionally, **firm-level "master detail" / detail-library practice** is well documented: structural engineering offices maintain reusable libraries of typical details (organized by material — wood, CMU, steel, concrete) that get pulled into a specific project's `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` sheets essentially unchanged from job to job **[confirmed]** ([Eng-Tips — Structural Engineering Detail Library](https://www.eng-tips.com/threads/structural-engineering-detail-library.413483/); published equivalents: [Standard Handbook of Structural Details for Building Construction](https://www.amazon.com/Standard-Handbook-Structural-Building-Construction/dp/0070463522), [Structural Details Manual](https://www.amazon.com/Structural-Details-Manual-David-Williams/dp/0070704430)). This independently corroborates that a `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` is a recognizably *reusable, project-agnostic* content type — reinforcing that it should not itself be a takeoff item; it's the *reference to* a template detail, from a location-specific plan/schedule, that becomes a takeoff item.

**Revised taxonomy recommendation:** keep the audit's proposed category *names* — they map reasonably well to both the NCS sheet-type-designator convention and to the EG-ICE classification axes — but restructure them as **two orthogonal dimensions instead of one flat enum**, matching the multi-axis approach the EG-ICE paper validates:

- **Sheet role** (mostly matches the audit's list): `TITLE_INDEX`, `GENERAL_NOTES`, `LEGEND`, `ABBREVIATIONS`, `SPECIFICATION_REFERENCE`, `FRAMING_PLAN`, `ELEVATION`, `SECTION`, `DETAIL`, `SCHEDULE` (member/connection/column — schedules deserve to be their own role given how differently they should be parsed, i.e., as keyed tables, from prose notes), `UNKNOWN`.
- **Takeoff-reachability** (new, and this is the axis that actually needs `TEMPLATE_DETAIL`): every `DETAIL` or `SECTION` region is additionally tagged `INSTANTIATED` (has at least one live, traceable plan/schedule reference pointing to it in this document set → counts toward quantity) or `TEMPLATE` (no traced reference found → present, buildable, real content, but not — yet, or ever — a located quantity). A detail doesn't stay permanently in one bucket; a `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` becomes `INSTANTIATED` the moment Section G's reference graph successfully traces a callout to it. This directly operationalizes the audit's insight and ties F to G.

### F.2 How do professional estimators actually convert typical/repeated details into a takeoff quantity today?

This is the more consequential research question for Estima3D's roadmap, because it tells us whether `TEMPLATE_DETAIL → instance count` is a solved manual workflow AI should mimic, or a genuinely hard step estimators struggle with too.

- **Framing plans are the primary counting surface; detail sheets are the specification surface.** *"The framing plan is the most important sheet for a steel estimator, showing a bird's-eye view of every beam, girder, and column on a given floor level"* while *"stairs, railings, embeds, lintels, and miscellaneous metals live in the specifications and general notes rather than on a framing plan."* **[reported-not-verified — single vendor source, but consistent with the audit's own finding that most real bent-plate callouts live on detail/section sheets, not plans]** ([SteelFlo — What Is a Steel Takeoff?](https://www.steelfloai.com/blog/what-is-a-steel-takeoff)).
- **Estimators explicitly cross-reference plan-to-detail as a manual, checklist-driven step**, and use **triangulation** (visual count vs. schedule count vs. weight/tonnage sanity-check) specifically to catch missed or double-counted detail-driven items: *"If all three methods do not align within a small tolerance, something is wrong. Reconcile before moving to pricing."* **[reported-not-verified — vendor blog, but describes a specific, checkable methodology rather than a vague claim]** ([Steel Takeoff Checklist](https://sketchdeck.ai/blog/steel-takeoff-checklist-what-every-estimator-should-verify/)). This is a strong signal: even skilled humans don't trust a single counting pass for detail-driven items — this argues Estima3D should likewise never treat a detail-reference-graph count as authoritative on its own, but should cross-validate it against an independent signal (e.g., a schedule row count, or a plan symbol count), mirroring the human triangulation practice.
- Manual takeoff for a "typical mid-size structural package" is reported to take **4–8 hours**, with detail cross-referencing described as a major share of that effort **[reported-not-verified]** (same source). This quantifies the labor cost Estima3D would be displacing if plan→detail reference resolution were automated — useful as a value-proposition data point, not as ground truth.
- **AISC's own layered-document model** confirms the plan→detail→schedule→shop-drawing chain is the *intended*, standardized information flow, not an artifact of any one office's habits: *"each layer references the next, with a steel beam sized on the framing plan, detailed in a section, connected by a connection schedule, and erected per the erection plan and shop drawings."* **[reported-not-verified, aggregated summary from search of AISC-adjacent sources, not a verbatim AISC quote]** ([AISC — Shop Detail Drawing Review/Approval Guidelines G-1.1-2020](https://www.aisc.org/globalassets/nsba/aashto-nsba-collab-docs/g-1.1-2020-shop-detail-drawing-review-approval-guidelines.pdf); summarized further at [TechDraw AI — Structural Steel Shop Drawings: AISC 303 Rules](https://techdrawai.com/blog/structural-steel-shop-drawings)).
- **Existing commercial tooling already treats plan→detail linking as a distinct, sellable feature**, independent of takeoff itself — i.e., the industry already recognizes "does every callout correctly point somewhere" as a discrete problem worth automating (see Section G for detail).
- **BIM's type/instance model is the cleanest existing analogy for TEMPLATE_DETAIL vs. instantiated quantity.** In BIM, the data hierarchy is `category → family → type → instance`, and a quantity takeoff can be run either "itemized per instance" or "summarized per type" **[confirmed]** ([Frame — BIM Quantity Takeoff glossary](https://www.bimframe.co/glossary/quantity-takeoff/)). A 2D PDF drawing set has no native type/instance data model — a detail is drawn once and referenced by convention, not by a database relationship — which is exactly the gap Section G's reference graph is meant to reconstruct. **[inferred]** This reframes `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` precisely: it is the PDF-world equivalent of a BIM **type/family definition**, and each traced plan callout to it is the equivalent of a BIM **instance**. That reframing is worth adopting explicitly in the ontology's naming, since it borrows a proven, well-understood industry data model rather than inventing new vocabulary.

**Conclusion for F:** The audit's proposed category list survives external scrutiny well, but should be split into an orthogonal (role × takeoff-reachability) model rather than one flat list, `SCHEDULE` types deserve to be pulled out as their own role rather than folded generically into content, and `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` maps directly and usefully onto the BIM type/instance distinction — which gives Estima3D a proven data model to imitate rather than a novel one to invent.

## G. Detail-reference graph

### G.1 Worked example, reasoned through

Framing plan references `4/S5.2` five times (five callout bubbles at five plan locations). Detail `4/S5.2` itself contains a `3/8" BENT PL` with dimensions and a piece mark, but *no location and no quantity of its own* — a detail sheet describes a condition, not an inventory.

**Should the system infer the plate definition from the detail and the instance count from the plan references, generating five bent-plate takeoff candidates?**

Yes, conditionally — this is the single most valuable structural inference Estima3D could add on top of the current per-token extraction pipeline, **provided** the count is corroborated rather than trusted blindly (per the F.2 triangulation finding above), because:

1. It matches exactly how a human estimator already does this task manually (Section F.2) — this is not proposing a new workflow, it's proposing automation of an existing, well-defined, checklist-driven one.
2. It directly resolves the audit's own strongest finding: *only 3.1–5.6% of plate/BENT hits occur on dedicated legend/notes pages; the large majority occur on detail/section sheets, referenced by callout bubble from framing plans, and the audit explicitly performed no plan-to-detail cross-reference following.* Section G is the natural next phase of exactly the gap the audit identified, not a speculative new direction.
3. Five references to the same detail is not automatically five identical physical plates — dimensions or orientation could vary slightly per instance if the detail itself parameterizes a dimension (e.g., "L = SEE PLAN"), which is why the *count* of references is a candidate-count signal, not automatically a *final* quantity, without also checking whether each referencing plan location carries its own overriding dimension. **[inferred]** This is a case where the E.2 precedence model directly interacts with G: a local plan annotation at one of the five callout locations should override the detail's generic dimension for that one instance, while the other four inherit the detail's value.

### G.2 Analogous approaches from research and adjacent industries

**BIM callout/reference modeling** already formalizes exactly this plan-condition → definition → instance relationship natively, but only inside a native BIM authoring tool, not from a flat PDF: a **reference callout** in Revit "points to an existing view instead of creating a new one... multiple reference callouts can point to the same view, ensuring consistency and reducing duplication," and the underlying relationship is maintained as live model data, not as text to be re-parsed **[confirmed]** ([Understanding Reference Callouts in Revit](https://bim-depot.com/blogs/revit-information-center/understanding-reference-callouts-in-revit); [Creating Detail Callouts for Drafting Views in Revit](https://www.nobledesktop.com/learn/revit/creating-detail-callouts-for-drafting-views)). This is the strongest available proof-of-concept that plan→detail is a well-modeled, well-understood *relationship type* in the industry — Estima3D's job is to reconstruct that relationship from PDF geometry/text when no native model exists, which is a harder, lossier version of a solved problem, not an unprecedented one.

**Commercial AEC document-QA tooling already builds exactly this kind of reference graph, as a standalone product feature**, independent of takeoff: a callout-detection product claims to "build a complete map of annotations and their targets," identify "every callout bubble, section marker, detail reference, keynote, and specification callout across all sheets," and "trace" each to its target, validating "the referenced sheet exists, the detail number is correct, and the content matches" — explicitly framed as **cross-sheet validation traced across the full set**, with a quoted (unverified) industry pain-point figure that *"approximately 20% of callouts reference wrong sheet or detail"* **[reported-not-verified — single vendor's marketing claim, no methodology disclosed; treat the 20% figure with skepticism, but the existence of a commercial market for this exact capability is itself the signal worth trusting]** ([Helonic — Annotation & Callout Detection](https://helonic.com/features/annotation-detection)). This is important evidence for Estima3D's prioritization decision: a competitor-adjacent product line already exists purely around *validating* the reference graph — which means Estima3D building the graph primarily to *drive quantity inference* (a further step beyond validation) would be a meaningful differentiator, not a redundant feature.

**P&ID (piping & instrumentation diagram) digitization is the closest mature academic/industrial analog** for symbol+text+cross-reference graph extraction in engineering drawings generally, and is considerably more mature as a research area than structural-drawing-specific work:
- End-to-end P&ID digitization pipelines are described as three explicit stages — **object recognition → topology reconstruction → digital generation** — where topology reconstruction is exactly the cross-reference/connectivity step Estima3D would need for plan→detail linking **[confirmed]** ([End-to-end digitization of image format P&IDs, J. Computational Design and Engineering](https://academic.oup.com/jcde/article/9/4/1298/6611631)).
- Reported symbol-recognition precision/recall in mature P&ID pipelines reaches **96.65%/96.40%**, with newer work citing **99.2% mAP** on symbol detection plus OCR-based tag extraction **[reported-not-verified — figures aggregated from search summaries of multiple papers, not independently verified against the primary sources]**, and the resulting structure is explicitly exported as a **graph** — "the enriched entity graph is exported as JSON, ISO 15926 RDF, or Neo4j-loadable Cypher script," with element connections captured as line-to-equipment, instrument-to-loop, and valve-to-line edges **[reported-not-verified, aggregated]**. This graph-export pattern (JSON/RDF/property-graph) is a directly reusable architectural template for a detail-reference graph: nodes = {plan location, callout bubble, detail sheet, detail entity/piece-mark}, edges = {"callout references detail," "detail defines entity," "plan location instantiates entity"}.
- A concrete, peer-reviewed implementation combining deep learning symbol/text detection with explicit **graph construction** for engineering diagrams exists in **Mani et al., "Automatic Digitization of Engineering Diagrams Using Deep Learning and Graph," CVPRW 2020** — cited via search summary as building a graph representation over recognized diagram elements, though the primary PDF could not be fetched directly (403 from the CVF open-access server) **[reported-not-verified — full text inaccessible at fetch time; citation retained because the paper is a real, findable, peer-reviewed CVPR workshop paper directly on-topic, but specific architectural claims about it should be re-verified before being treated as design requirements]** ([Mani et al., CVPRW 2020 — paper listing](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPRW_2020/papers/w8/Mani_Automatic_Digitization_of_Engineering_Diagrams_Using_Deep_Learning_and_Graph_CVPRW_2020_paper.pdf)).

**Symbol spotting is the well-studied academic subfield underlying the "find the callout bubble" step** of any such graph, with an active, current line of work: FloorPlanCAD's panoptic symbol-spotting benchmark, and follow-on 2024–2025 work (SymPoint, Text-Enhanced Panoptic Symbol Spotting) explicitly frames the task as **jointly detecting countable symbol instances and reading associated text** in CAD/vector drawings **[confirmed — these are real, current arXiv papers with matching abstracts]** ([FloorPlanCAD](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.07147); [SymPoint Revolutionized](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.01928); [Text-Enhanced Panoptic Symbol Spotting in CAD Drawings](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.11091)). Two caveats: (a) this line of work targets **floor plans**, not structural-steel framing/detail sheets specifically — the domain gap (door/window/wall symbols vs. detail bubbles/piece marks) is real and unquantified, so transferability should be treated as a hypothesis, not a given **[inferred, flagged as a gap]**; (b) it operates on **vector CAD data**, which is a good match for Estima3D's born-digital-vector PDF corpus (per the audit, all 262 pages were born-digital vector text) rather than a mismatch requiring rasterization-and-CV, which is a meaningful practical advantage.

**No structural-steel-specific published research on plan-to-detail cross-reference graph construction was found** in this search — the closest published analogs are P&ID topology reconstruction and general CAD symbol spotting, both adjacent-domain rather than on-domain. This is itself a finding: Estima3D would be working close to, but ahead of, the published research frontier on this specific sub-problem, i.e., there is no existing tool or paper to simply adopt — the P&ID and BIM analogs establish *feasibility and pattern*, not a ready-made solution. **[inferred from absence across ~15 targeted searches]**

### G.3 Major or lower-priority architecture component?

**This should be a major, but second-phase, architecture component — not a peripheral feature.**

Reasoning:

1. **It targets the audit's largest quantified gap, not a marginal one.** The audit found the *majority* of real bent-plate (and by extension, likely other detail-driven fabricated-item) callouts live on detail sheets reached only by plan cross-reference, with essentially 0% of that path currently traced. A system that cannot follow plan→detail references is, per the audit's own numbers, blind to the majority of one entire class of real quantities — this is not an edge case.
2. **It is well-precedented as a distinct, valuable capability independent of takeoff** — commercial tooling (Helonic) already sells reference-graph *validation* alone as a product, and BIM natively models the same relationship as a first-class citizen (reference callouts, type/instance). Estima3D would not be inventing an unproven capability; it would be reconstructing, from flat PDF geometry, a relationship that better-instrumented workflows (native BIM) already get for free — which is a defensible, well-motivated R&D investment rather than a speculative one.
3. **But it should sequence after the more basic legend/precedence work in Sections E and F**, because the reference graph's value is capped by the quality of the entity extraction and precedence model feeding it: a plate defined ambiguously inside a `TEMPLATE_DETAIL` (wrong precedence resolution, wrong dimension picked) produces five confidently-wrong candidates instead of one, which is a worse failure mode than not generating candidates at all. The human-estimator triangulation practice found in F.2 (visual count vs. schedule vs. tonnage sanity check) should be built in from the start as a required cross-check, not treated as a later hardening pass — an ungated "reference count = quantity" pipeline risks systematically over- or under-counting whenever a callout bubble is misread, a detail is redlined/superseded, or an instance carries a local override.
4. **The nearest mature research analog (P&ID digitization) took years of dedicated, domain-specific work to reach production-grade accuracy** even in a more topologically explicit domain (P&ID connectivity is drawn as literal lines between symbols; structural plan→detail references are drawn as *symbolic* bubble+number pairs requiring text/number matching across sheets, a comparatively harder linking problem with weaker built-in visual structure). This argues for treating G as a genuine multi-phase R&D effort — start with high-confidence, well-formed callout bubbles (`N/Sheet#` format, matching a detail title block exactly) before attempting the harder long tail (implicit "SEE PLAN" references, superseded/renumbered sheets, multi-hop references).

## H. Structural semantic ontology

### H.1 The problem, restated precisely

The audit (confirmed, `legend_aware_extraction_audit.md` §H, §J) found that Estima3D's current pipeline has exactly one output slot per token — `category` — and every classifier (`entity_taxonomy.classify_category`, `engineering_object_filter.classify_engineering_object`) writes into that single slot. Real drawing text routinely needs several independent facts about the same token, not one:

- **Catalog/profile identity** — is this string a resolvable AISC (or project-substituted) profile, and what is it? (`W8x10`, confirmed AISC-grammar; or `"W8"` on GCDC, confirmed §D to mean `W8x10` only via a project-declared substitution.)
- **Object class** — what kind of physical thing is it, independent of role? (plate, angle, HSS, W-shape, fastener.)
- **Functional role** — what job does it do in the structure? (brace, stair stringer, closure, pour stop, seat, stiffener, connection plate — confirmed vocabulary, §D "Contextual roles actually found.")
- **Fabrication modifier** — how is the catalog/class item altered from its as-rolled state? (`BENT` — confirmed, 112 instances, §E; `CURVED`/`SLOPED`/`CAMBERED`/`CONT`/`GALV` — **inferred** as a plausible closed set by analogy to `BENT`, not independently confirmed in this corpus.)
- **Dimensions, and their completeness/source** — full width×length×thickness, thickness-only (confirmed dominant pattern for plate-family callouts, §E), or none at all.
- **Quantity status** — fully specified and takeoff-ready vs. real-but-undimensioned/contractor-designed (confirmed real examples: `"GUSSET AND BENT PL TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"`, §F.4) vs. `template_detail` (a detail-sheet definition referenced by callout bubble elsewhere, confirmed §G/§I as the dominant pattern for bent-plate evidence in this corpus).
- **Delegated-design status** — is this element's engineering design retained by the Engineer of Record or explicitly handed to the contractor?

The question for this section is not "should these be separate fields" (the audit already argues yes) but **which of these axes are structural facts that any serious steel-modeling system has independently discovered it must keep separate, versus which are novel to the drawing-interpretation problem** — because if mature CAD/detailing/BIM tools already separate an axis, that's strong outside evidence the axis is real, not an artifact of this corpus's 7 PDFs.

### H.2 What buildingSMART/IFC separates — and why

IFC's kernel enforces a **type/occurrence split** as a first-class modeling paradigm, independent of steel specifically. `IfcTypeObject` — "The object type defines the specific information about a type... common to all occurrences of this type... refers to the specific level of the well-recognized generic – specific – occurrence modeling paradigm" — is attached to individual instances via the `IfcRelDefinesByType` relationship, not by copying data ([IfcTypeObject, IFC4.3.2.0](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcTypeObject.htm); [IfcTypeProduct](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4/ADD1/HTML/schema/ifckernel/lexical/ifctypeproduct.htm)) — confirmed by direct reading of the schema documentation.

Within that split, IFC further separates **cross-sectional geometry from the physical element and from the element's type**: `IfcProfileDef` is "the supertype of all definitions of standard and arbitrary profiles within IFC, used to define a standard set of commonly used section profiles by their parameters or by their explicit curve geometry" ([IfcProfileDef](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/DEV/IFC4_2/FINAL/HTML/schema/ifcprofileresource/lexical/ifcprofiledef.htm), confirmed) — this is the direct IFC analog of "catalog/profile identity." An `IfcBeam` or `IfcPlate` occurrence gets its profile via `IfcMaterialProfileSetUsage`, which is deliberately kept as a *usage/positioning* layer on top of the profile, not folded into it: "Material profile set usage defines... the offset from the 'Axis' reference curve according to cardinal point" ([Material Profile Set Usage](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_1/FINAL/HTML/schema/templates/material-profile-set-usage.htm), confirmed). Object class is separated again: `IfcPlate` is defined independent of material — "the definition of IfcPlate is material independent and specific material information shall be handled by using IfcRelAssociatesMaterial to assign a material specification to the IfcPlate" ([IfcPlate, IFC4.3.2.0](https://standards.buildingsmart.org/IFC/RELEASE/IFC4_3/HTML/lexical/IfcPlate.htm), confirmed) — i.e., "this is a plate" (object class) is a fact independent of "this plate is A36 steel" (material) and independent of "this plate is 3/8" thick" (dimension), each attached through its own relationship object rather than as fields on one record.

Fabrication modifiers specifically have a documented, separate home too: camber is not a property of the profile or the type, it is carried as displacement data on a structural analysis member — "the camber of a beam may be defined by assigning an `IfcStructuralCurveMember` with displacement coordinates, and multiple sets of camber ordinates may be provided that are qualified by the particular load case" ([IfcBeam discussion, buildingSMART](https://github.com/buildingSMART/IFC4.x-development/blob/master/docs/schemas/shared/IfcSharedBldgElements/Entities/IfcBeam.md), reported/confirmed via search summary of the primary GitHub doc — not independently re-fetched page-by-page). Fastening/connection elements are separated from the members they join: `IfcMechanicalFastener` (bolts) and a separate `IfcFastener` (welds) are distinct entities from `IfcPlate`, used together to represent a connection assembly rather than one entity absorbing all of it ("SCIA Engineer supports export of flat steel connection parts, cleats and stiffeners to IFC like independent plates (`IfcPlate`), an information about a weld (`IfcFastener` entity) and bolts (`IfcMechanicalFastener` entity)" — [SCIA IFC manual](https://help.scia.net/22.0/en/data_transfer/ifc/ifc_manual.htm), reported, not independently verified against the SCIA product itself).

**What IFC does *not* separately model, confirmed by absence:** none of the entities found in this research carry anything resembling "quantity status" (real-but-undimensioned/contractor-designed) or "delegated-design status." This makes sense — IFC models an object that has already been designed and is being described geometrically; it has no vocabulary for "this object is known to exist and its role is known, but its size is deliberately not yet fixed by the document." That is a genuine gap between what mature BIM schemas solve and what Estima3D needs to solve, because Estima3D operates on **construction documents**, which sit temporally *before* delegated design is complete — a different problem than describing a finished model.

### H.3 What Tekla Structures, SDS/2, and Advance Steel separate

These three are steel-specific detailing tools (not generic BIM schemas), so they are closer analogs, but proprietary — the point here is only to check whether their internal data models independently rediscovered the same separations.

- **Tekla Structures** keeps a **profile catalog** as a shared, named library — "the profile catalog contains information on profiles, their rules and types... [containing] standard, environment-specific profiles and generic parametric profiles" ([Customize the profile catalog](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-structures/2021/mod_profiles), confirmed) — and explicitly supports attaching **user-defined attributes to profiles as a separate layer**, distinct from the profile's own geometric identity: "you can add your own attributes to profiles... specify paint layer thickness, define the maximum grain size of concrete, sort out different profile types by material, or create profile aliases for converting imperial profiles to metric" ([Add user attributes to profiles](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-structures/2022/mod_catalogs_adding_user_defined_attributes_to_profile), confirmed). The explicit mention of "profile aliases for converting imperial profiles to metric" is a striking direct analog to GCDC's `"W8"=W8x10` substitution table (§D) — Tekla's own documentation treats "an alias resolves to a real profile" as a first-class, expected need, not an edge case. Separately, **custom components** encode reusable, parametric *functional-role* assemblies (a connection type, a stiffener detail) independent of which specific profile fills each slot — the Custom Component Wizard requires defining component properties distinct from any one profile instance ([Custom component properties](https://support.tekla.com/doc/tekla-structures/2025/det_cc_custom_component_wizard_properties), confirmed existence of the concept, not independently exercised).
- **SDS/2** models per-member fabrication data as something read per-object even when many objects share a mark, and keeps job-wide fabrication *settings* (formatting/detailing rules) in a separate store from individual member data: "steel design codes are in job setup, where specifications about how to format details are in fabrication settings... [and] the API provides all member data" as a distinct, per-member structure ([SDS2 Database Structure](https://developer.sds2.com/2022/net/tutorial/databasestructure.html), reported from search summary). Connection design is modeled as its own class hierarchy — "specifications to design specific connection types are available as a base class with all options found on derived connection types" (same source, reported) — i.e., functional/connection role is a distinct object graph from the member profile, not a tag on it.
- **Advance Steel** confirms the sharpest version of the "object class ≠ fabrication data" separation found in this research: "Object Fabrication Data is always available and is defined on each object, even if some objects have the same Part Mark" ([Fabrication data tab, Advance Steel 2017](https://knowledge.autodesk.com/support/advance-steel/learn-explore/caas/CloudHelp/cloudhelp/2017/ENU/AdvSteel-3Dmodeling/files/GUID-1022DBA8-E745-4B0A-9906-F90B14509C12-htm.html), confirmed). This is direct outside evidence for keeping fabrication modifier as its own axis rather than folding it into object class or catalog identity: two objects can be catalog-identical and mark-identical and still carry different fabrication facts.

### H.4 What Revit separates (type vs. instance parameters)

Revit's family/type/instance model is the cleanest, most widely documented version of the type–occurrence split for a non-specialist audience: "Type parameters apply to every element of that family type... section size and material are classic type parameters," while "instance parameters apply to a single placed element... such as a custom Z-offset or a specific mark number" ([Revit Tutorial – Type vs Instance Parameters](https://revitstructureblog.wordpress.com/2019/05/02/revit-tutorial-type-vs-instance-parameters/), confirmed). Shared parameters exist specifically to let the *same* parameter (e.g. "Section Name," "Section Type") be scheduled and tagged consistently *across* families ([s15studio, Revit Structural Framing Explained](https://www.s15studio.com/post/revit-structural-framing-explained-for-engineers), reported). This maps cleanly onto Estima3D's problem: **catalog/profile identity and object class behave like type-level facts** (shared across every occurrence of that callout on the sheet set), while **dimensions-as-given, quantity status, and delegated-design status behave like instance-level facts** (they can differ occurrence-to-occurrence even when the catalog identity is the same — a `3/8" BENT PL` at one edge condition may be fully dimensioned while the "same" callout elsewhere is contractor-deferred).

### H.5 What AISC's own standards say about identity vs. fabrication

The AISC Shapes Database and its EDI naming convention define catalog identity as a closed, syntactic grammar (`W`, `M`, `S`, `HP`, `C`, `MC`, `L`, `WT`, `MT`, `ST`, `2L`, `HSS`, `PIPE` prefixes plus dimension strings — [AISC Shapes Database v16.0](https://www.aisc.org/aisc/publications/steel-construction-manual/aisc-shapes-database-v160/); [Naming Convention for Structural Steel Products for EDI](https://www.aisc.org/globalassets/aisc/manual/v15.0-shapes-database/naming-convention-for-structural-steel-products-for-use-in-electronic-data-interchange-edi.pdf), confirmed existence and general format, PDF body not independently re-fetched). Nothing in that naming grammar encodes bending, curving, camber, or coating — those live in **separate** AISC publications: [AISC Design Guide 36, Design Considerations for Camber](https://pardis-elm.ir/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/AISC-Design-Guide-36.pdf) and Design Guide 33 (curved members), and industry fabricator literature distinguishes camber ("a curve in the vertical plane... 1/2 to 3 inches over 40ft") from sweep/curving ("horizontal plane... from 1000ft radius to 10ft radius") as physically and procedurally distinct fabrication operations from straight rolling ([Curving and Cambering Steel Sections, The Chicago Curve](https://www.cmrp.com/blog/bending/curving-and-cambering-steel-sections.html), reported). **This is outside confirmation that "fabrication modifier" is a real, industry-recognized axis independent of catalog identity** — it is not an artifact this audit invented from 7 PDFs; AISC itself publishes fabrication-modifier information in documents entirely separate from the shapes database that defines identity.

### H.6 What the industry's own contracts say about delegated-design status

"Delegated-design status" is not an ontology invention either — it is standard AISC/AIA contract vocabulary. Under ANSI/AISC 303, "Option 3" connection design is explicitly a case where "the EOR delegates design of the connections to a licensed engineer working for the structural steel fabricator" ([Delegating Connection Design, Harman Group/IMEG](https://harmangroup.com/blog/delegating-connection-design/), reported); more broadly, "delegated design involves a division of responsibility between the design professional... and the contractor, with the contractor taking on design responsibility for a specific element of the project... [while] the design professional retains overall responsibility for the project's performance specifications" ([AIA Contract Documents, Delegated Design](https://learn.aiacontracts.com/articles/what-contractors-need-to-know-about-delegated-design/), confirmed by direct read). Critically, deferred-submittal guidance is explicit that this is *not* the same as abandoning design responsibility: the 2009 IBC defines deferred submittals as "portions of the design that are not submitted at the time of the (permit) application and that are to be submitted to the building official within a specified period," and California DSA guidance clarifies "deferred approval does not mean that the A/E of Record may defer the design of the component to the contractor" — the EOR remains "responsible for designing connections to the structure for all deferred approval components" even when the component itself is engineered later ([Deferred Submittals, STRUCTURE magazine](https://www.structuremag.org/article/deferred-submittals/), confirmed by direct read). This maps precisely onto the audit's real evidence — `"GUSSET AND BENT PL TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"` (§F.4) is not an ambiguous or missing fact, it is the drawing correctly recording a real, industry-standard delegation state that a takeoff system needs to represent as its own status, not as "dimension: none."

## I. Drawing Language Profile — refined schema

### I.1 What kind of document-AI technique is this, really?

The Drawing Language Profile (DLP), as sketched in the audit (§L), is a per-document, self-referential vocabulary table extracted once and then consulted repeatedly. Several existing research threads are adjacent, but none is an exact match:

- **Ontology/schema induction.** The general framing — deriving structure (a vocabulary of terms and their meanings) from unstructured text without a predefined ontology — is exactly what schema/ontology induction research studies. Early work used Markov-logic-based unsupervised induction ([Unsupervised Ontology Induction from Text, Poon & Domingos, ACL 2010](https://aclanthology.org/P10-1031.pdf), confirmed as a real, citable paper via search); more recent LLM-based variants exist, e.g. `INCSCHEMA`, which "decomposes the schema induction task into a series of simple questions through incremental prompting and verification" — notably already pairing generation with verification, which is close to what §I.3 below proposes ([Open-Domain Hierarchical Event Schema Induction by Incremental Prompting and Verification](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.01972), reported from search summary), and `LLMs4OL`, a zero-shot-prompting approach to ontology learning with LLMs (reported, not independently fetched). **Difference from DLP:** ontology induction typically targets a general, reusable ontology across many documents; DLP is deliberately narrow and single-document/single-project, closer to *instance* induction than *class* induction.
- **Few-shot document adaptation / metadata-conditioned extraction.** This research area studies how to make an extractor generalize to a new document layout or domain from very few examples, e.g. `FREDo`, a few-shot document-level relation-extraction benchmark built on DocRED/SciERC ([FREDo](https://aclanthology.org/2022.naacl-main.421/), reported), or `MetaIE`, which distills "a meta model from LLM for all kinds of information extraction tasks" to give a better few-shot starting point ([MetaIE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2404.00457), reported). **Difference from DLP:** these techniques adapt a *model* to a new document type using a handful of labeled examples; DLP does not adapt a model at all — it extracts a *data table* (the profile) that a fixed, deterministic downstream classifier then consults. DLP is closer to what these papers would call the metadata/conditioning signal than to the adaptation mechanism itself.
- **Schema linking.** In text-to-SQL, schema linking is "a method to link the phrases in a given question with the column names or the table names in the database schema" so a downstream parser only reasons over relevant, correctly-named schema elements (reported, general definition synthesized across multiple 2024–2025 papers: [RSL-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00073), [Extractive Schema Linking for Text-to-SQL](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.17174)). This is a genuinely close conceptual analog: the DLP's job, once built, is to let a downstream token classifier "link" a raw string (`"W8"`) to the correct catalog referent (`W8x10`) using project-specific naming — structurally the same move as linking a question phrase to a column name using database-specific naming. **Difference:** schema linking operates against an already-known, externally-defined schema (the database); DLP has to *build* its own schema from the document first, then link against it. DLP is closer to "induce the schema, then do schema linking against what you induced," a two-stage composition the text-to-SQL literature doesn't need because the schema is normally given.
- **Weak supervision / data programming.** Snorkel's core idea — subject-matter experts write noisy, imperfect **labeling functions**, and a generative model resolves their agreements/disagreements into training labels without hand-labeling ([Snorkel: Rapid Training Data Creation with Weak Supervision](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10160), confirmed) — is the closest methodological analog to what an LLM-proposed, corpus-validated DLP rule actually is: **a labeling function proposed by an LLM instead of a human SME**, whose reliability still has to be estimated empirically (in Snorkel's case, from agreement statistics across many labeling functions; in DLP's case, from how often the rule's predicted pattern actually recurs in the same document, §I.2 below).
- **Evidence-grounded attribution.** RARR — "automatically find[ing] attribution for the output of any text generation model and post-edit[ing] the output to fix unsupported content" using retrieval ([RARR, ACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.910/), confirmed) — is architecturally the right shape for DLP's validation step, just with the retrieval corpus narrowed from "the web" to "the rest of this one PDF."

**Inferred synthesis:** DLP is best described as **single-document ontology induction, validated as weak-supervision labeling functions via intra-document evidence retrieval** — a composition of three existing ideas rather than a novel one. That composition itself (induce → treat as noisy labeling functions → validate against the same source) does not appear to have an established name in the literature surveyed here; it is closest in spirit to `INCSCHEMA`'s "prompting and verification" loop, but applied to structured drafting-language rules rather than event schemas.

### I.2 How should a proposed rule be validated?

Concretely, using the audit's own worked example (§G, §M.2): the LLM reads GCDC p.5 and proposes `member_size_substitutions: {"W8": "W8x10", ...}`. Validation should not stop at "does this look like a plausible JSON object" (schema-shape validity) — it must check "is this substitution actually load-bearing information, i.e., does the pattern it claims to define actually recur." A concrete, deterministic procedure:

1. **Extract the trigger and target strings** from the proposed rule (`"W8"` → `W8x10`).
2. **Regex-scan the rest of the same document** (all pages except the source page) for literal or near-literal occurrences of the trigger string in a member-callout context (e.g. `\bW8\b` not immediately followed by digits, distinguishing a bare `"W8"` from an already-complete `W8X10`).
3. **Classify each hit**: *consistent* (a bare `W8` used exactly where the profile grammar would otherwise reject or misread it), *contradicting* (a `W8` used in a context that implies a different resolution, e.g. adjacent to a different explicit size), or *unrelated* (false-positive substring match, e.g. inside another word).
4. **Compute a support count and a contradiction count**, and derive `validation_status`: `validated` (support ≥ 1, contradictions = 0), `contradicted` (contradictions > 0 — surface for human review rather than silently picking a side), or `unsupported_not_activated` (support = 0 — exactly the GCDC case as currently found by this audit, §G: *"Not independently re-verified on GCDC's own framing plans in this pass... flagged requires_engineer_review"* — i.e., **this audit's own unresolved question is precisely the case an automated validator needs to catch**, and by its own finding, the rule should sit at `validated_status: unsupported_not_activated` until someone checks).

This is a direct, self-consistency-flavored technique: rather than sampling multiple LLM outputs and keeping the majority answer ([Self-Consistency Improves Chain of Thought Reasoning, Wang et al., 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171), confirmed), DLP validation samples multiple *occurrences in the source document* and keeps the rule only if independent evidence converges — the same "marginalize over multiple independent signals" logic, applied to document evidence instead of model samples. It is also methodologically close to recent work on **provenance-conditioned confidence for document extraction**: a 2026 paper on selective risk control in document extraction found that "support-bin, the pre-specified grounding-derived provenance taxonomy, wins every rigor tier" for calibrating whether an extracted field should be trusted, but that this advantage is "model-scoped, collapsing under weaker extractors" ([Valid Per-Field Selective Risk Control for Document Extraction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14639), confirmed by direct fetch of the paper). Two practical implications for DLP: (a) grounding/support-based confidence is a real, currently-published, empirically-validated signal — not an ad hoc heuristic — and (b) its benefit is not guaranteed to transfer automatically if the underlying extraction model changes, so DLP's validation logic should be re-calibrated (or at least re-checked) whenever the extraction/classification model is swapped, not assumed to keep working. A second, directly relevant 2026 system ("Guardian Parser Pack") implements almost exactly the propose→validate composition recommended here for a different domain: LLM output is "treated as candidates subject to deterministic checks" rather than authoritative, with a validator-guided repair loop as "a safeguard rather than... a contributor to the reported performance gains" ([LLM-based Schema-Guided Extraction and Validation](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06571), confirmed by direct fetch) — external, working confirmation that "LLM proposes, deterministic code disposes" is a viable production pattern, not just a theoretical nicety.

### I.3 How should rules be scoped?

The audit's own evidence already supplies the scoping dimension that matters most: **page role**. §I of the audit found that legend/cover-sheet content is only 3.1–5.6% of all plate-family/BENT hits, with the overwhelming majority occurring on typical-detail sheets — i.e., a rule extracted from a legend page needs an explicit statement of *where else it is allowed to fire*, because "legend page" and "the pages the rule actually governs" are not the same set. Concretely:

- Every rule should carry `"scope": {"applies_to_page_roles": [...], "source_pages": [...]}`, populated from the same `page_roles` map the audit's §L schema already proposes (`legend_and_general_notes`, `typical_detail`, `framing_detail`).
- Member-size substitution rules (like `"W8"=W8x10`) are natural **project-wide** rules — nothing in the GCDC evidence suggests they'd be sheet-type-specific — but connection-type vocabulary rules (like the BENT PLATE allowed/prohibited nuance, §I.4 below) are conditioned on *what kind of connection* is being discussed, which is closer to a **framing-detail-scoped** fact than a whole-document fact, since the audit found bent-plate evidence concentrated on detail sheets, never framing plans directly (§G).
- A revision/addendum axis is also visible in the corpus's own metadata, though not independently verified as changing rule content: the Sidwell filename itself is `03 - SFSLS_251029_ISSUED FOR BID_2A_STRUCTURAL complied thru add 2.pdf` (confirmed filename, audit §B), meaning this project's issued set already incorporates at least one addendum. **Inferred, not confirmed:** addenda can add or revise general notes, so a DLP built from one page of a multi-addendum set should record which issuance/addendum state the source page reflects and treat a later addendum's notes as taking precedence over an earlier page's notes if they conflict — this was not tested in the audit and should be a flagged assumption, not a hard rule, until checked against a real addendum-revision case.

### I.4 How should conflicting rules be resolved?

The audit's GCDC finding is the direct worked example: on the same page, `BENT PLATE SHEAR CONNECTIONS` appears as an *allowed* type (in a list of connection types requiring contractor design-table submittal) and `BENT PLATE CONNECTIONS WELDED TO THE SUPPORTING MEMBER` appears as *prohibited* elsewhere on the same page (both confirmed quotes, audit §E). This is not noise to be resolved into one boolean — the audit is explicit that collapsing this into a flat "is bent plate allowed: yes/no" fact would be a real information loss, and flags it as an open engineering question (§N Q5) whether the distinction (bolted-vs-welded) is meaningful or boilerplate inconsistency.

The correct schema behavior, given that ambiguity is itself unresolved, is: **never merge two rule statements about the same term into one boolean; store each as its own conditional rule entry with its own quote, page, and condition string, and let a human (or a downstream connection-detailing step) resolve applicability per instance.** This is consistent with the weak-supervision framing above — Snorkel's label model is designed specifically to represent *disagreement* between labeling functions statistically rather than force a premature consensus; DLP should do the analogous thing at the schema level (store both conditional facts) rather than at the confidence-averaging level (which would silently produce a meaningless 50%-confidence "sometimes allowed" flag).

### I.5 Immutable or evolving?

**Recommendation (inferred, not sourced from a specific paper): versioned and append-only per project, not mutated in place.** Concretely: each DLP is generated once per source-document state (a specific issuance/addendum), stored as an immutable snapshot with its own version id, and a new DLP version is created — never edited over the old one — if new evidence (a later addendum, a corrected legend page, an engineer's manual correction from §N-style review) changes a rule. This is standard practice for any system where provenance and auditability matter more than storage efficiency (config-as-data / feature-store versioning is a broadly established engineering pattern, not specific to this domain), and it directly serves the explainability goal the audit already prioritizes (§K: "quote the exact legend sentence that justified this rule"): a reviewer should always be able to ask "what did the system believe about this project on date X" and get a real, frozen answer, not a mutated table with no history.

### I.6 Confidence score and source-page evidence — yes, and what should feed it

Every rule entry should carry: (a) a raw `extraction_confidence` from the LLM proposal step itself (how syntactically clean/unambiguous the source sentence was), (b) a `validation_status` from §I.2's corpus-scan step, and (c) a derived `combined_confidence` that is *not* just the LLM's self-reported number — per the selective-risk-control paper above, naive confidence-threshold calibration on a single split "silently violates" real risk control, and document-clustering effects alone can make a naively-calibrated threshold overshoot its nominal risk in roughly half of realistic calibration splits ([Valid Per-Field Selective Risk Control for Document Extraction](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.14639), confirmed). Practically, that means: don't ship a single scalar confidence per rule based on the LLM call alone; combine (extraction confidence, corpus-support count, contradiction count) into `combined_confidence`, and treat any rule with `validated_status: unsupported_not_activated` as **not eligible to influence classification at all**, regardless of how confident the LLM sounded — confidence-gating on the LLM's own score, without grounding, is exactly the failure mode this literature warns about.

### J.1 The four architectures, compared

| Dimension | A. No LLM (deterministic only) | B. LLM as legend/notes parser only | C. LLM proposes → deterministic corpus-verification → activate | D. LLM at inference time on ambiguous tokens |
|---|---|---|---|---|

### J.2 Is C's advantage a real pattern in the literature, or just convenient for this corpus?

Independent of the audit's own conclusion, the broader literature on trustworthy LLM extraction converges on the same "generate freely, verify against ground truth cheaply" shape from several unrelated directions, which is stronger evidence than any one of them alone:

- **Weak supervision** established, years before LLMs were involved, that noisy, individually-unreliable labeling signals can be combined into a trustworthy label set *if* their agreement/support is measured statistically rather than trusted individually ([Snorkel](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10160), confirmed). An LLM-proposed DLP rule is structurally a labeling function; corpus re-scan support is the modern, single-source analog of Snorkel's cross-labeling-function agreement statistic.
- **Program synthesis from examples** (FlashFill and its generalization) solved a related but distinct problem — synthesizing a transformation *program* from a handful of input/output examples, using "search and verification" components where a Counterexample-Guided Inductive Synthesis (CEGIS) loop "uses the output from verification to refine the specification given to search" ([FlashMeta](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/wp-content/uploads/2016/12/oopsla15-pbe.pdf), confirmed). This is a closer analog to Architecture C than it might first appear: DLP's "propose a rule, then check it against the rest of the document" loop is a one-shot, non-iterative version of the same search+verify shape — the LLM proposes a candidate generalization (the rule), and the corpus re-scan plays the verifier's role, just without feeding counterexamples back for refinement (a plausible future extension, not proposed as necessary here).
- **Retrieval-grounded attribution.** RARR explicitly frames the problem as "a user cannot easily determine whether [LLM] outputs are trustworthy because most language models do not have any built-in mechanism for attribution to external evidence," and solves it by researching and revising claims against retrieved evidence, requiring "only a handful of training examples" to do so ([RARR](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-long.910/), confirmed). DLP's corpus re-scan is RARR's idea with the retrieval corpus narrowed to "the rest of this one document" — a much easier retrieval problem (no ranking, no open-domain ambiguity) than RARR's original web-search setting, which if anything makes the technique *more* reliable in this application than in RARR's own.
- **Working 2026 systems already ship this pattern**, not just propose it in theory: the Guardian Parser Pack treats "LLM results... as candidates subject to deterministic checks," with schema validation and repair as "a safeguard rather than a contributor" to accuracy — i.e., the deterministic layer is what actually earns trust, and the LLM is explicitly demoted to a proposal generator ([Guardian Parser Pack](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06571), confirmed).
- **Self-consistency** ([Wang et al. 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.11171), confirmed) is a complementary, optional second layer: sampling the LLM's legend-page extraction multiple times and keeping only rules that recur across samples adds a second, independent confidence signal on top of corpus-grounding, cheaply, since it only costs extra LLM calls on the small legend-page input, not on the whole document.

No source found in this research argues for skipping verification and trusting LLM output directly on a comparably high-stakes structured-extraction task (Architecture B alone, or D). Every relevant 2025–2026 system found in this search that discusses reliability explicitly adds a deterministic or grounding-based check *after* LLM generation, not instead of it.

### J.3 Recommendation

**Architecture A is a precondition, not an alternative** — the audit already shows the thickness-only regex fix and other deterministic gaps (§H, §M.1) explain most of the corpus's real loss and cost nothing to fix; this should happen regardless of any LLM decision, and doing it first also shrinks the residual problem that any LLM architecture would need to solve.

**Architecture D is not supported by any evidence gathered here, internal or external, and should not be pursued now.** The one internal data point (partner's pilot) is confounded exactly the way this section's literature review would predict (no OCR/vision separation, no grounding backstop, malformed structured output already observed), and every dimension in the comparison table above — cost, latency, hallucination risk, structured-output reliability, privacy payload size, maintainability — is worse than B or C with no offsetting accuracy evidence to justify it.

**Architecture C is the correct target, and the case for it is not merely "the audit's author already suspected this" — it is convergent with weak supervision, program-synthesis verification loops, retrieval-grounded attribution, and at least one working 2026 production-style system, all independently arriving at the same shape: let the LLM generate freely (that's what LLMs are good at), and let cheap, deterministic, auditable code decide what gets trusted (that's what LLMs are bad at and code is good at).** Architecture B is not a wrong alternative so much as an *incomplete* version of C — B is the generation half of C without the verification half, and every dimension where C outperforms B in the table above (hallucination risk, determinism, explainability) is precisely the dimension the verification step adds. The recommended narrowest defensible LLM role, stated as a job description: **read a small, bounded amount of legend/general-notes text once per document; propose structured candidate rules, each with a source quote and page; never write directly to any classification output; and let deterministic code decide, per rule, whether the rest of the document actually supports it before that rule is allowed to touch a single token's classification.**

## K. VLM role

### K.1 Framing

The question is not "does Estima3D need a VLM" in the abstract — it's: for each specific interpretation problem the 7-PDF corpus actually contains, is the information already present in the PDF's text layer and vector path geometry (extractable today, no rasterization), or is it only recoverable by rendering the page to pixels and doing genuine visual/perceptual reasoning over them? PDF content streams for vector-native drawings carry explicit line/curve/arc primitives, stroke width, dash arrays, fill patterns, and per-glyph text position — this is a stronger starting position than "a photo of a drawing," and the literature on document AI and CAD-symbol recognition increasingly treats "operate on the vector/graph structure directly" as a distinct, often stronger, alternative to "rasterize and use a vision model." A 2023 survey of document layout analysis notes explicitly that the vector primitives underlying digital-native PDFs are typically thrown away in favor of pixel-based analysis even when they are readily available and more precise — this is presented as a real, current gap in tooling practice, not a solved problem **[confirmed]** ([A Graphical Approach to Document Layout Analysis](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.02051)).

### K.2 Problem-by-problem breakdown

| Problem | Solvable from vector/text alone? | Why / how | Genuinely needs pixels? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Bend geometry / fold-line representation in section views** | **Yes, largely** | A folded plate in a vector-native section view is drawn as literal connected line segments at an angle; the angle discontinuity between consecutive vector path segments is directly computable from the PDF's own path-command geometry (line endpoints, control points) without ever rendering a pixel. This is the same primitive-level signal CAD-symbol-spotting systems like CADTransformer already key on — panoptic symbol spotting on CAD drawings is explicitly framed around sampling and classifying vector/graphic entities, not raw pixels **[confirmed]** ([CADTransformer, CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Fan_CADTransformer_Panoptic_Symbol_Spotting_Transformer_for_CAD_Drawings_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)). What genuinely resists a pure-geometry rule is *distinguishing a real fabrication bend-line from an ordinary hidden-line/section-cut convention line drawn at a similar angle* — that's a drafting-convention classification problem, which is closer to a symbol/pattern-matching task on the vector graph than a pixel-perception task. |
| **Leader-arrow ownership/interpretation** | **Yes, mostly, but this is the corpus's own confirmed hard case** | Geometrically, a leader is a vector path (often with an arrowhead sub-path) whose endpoint sits near a text-span bounding box and whose other endpoint sits near a geometry element or another text span — both endpoints and all bounding boxes are already extractable, so ownership is in principle a proximity/ray-intersection problem over data Estima3D already has. But a 2026 benchmark built specifically to test this found that current frontier VLMs *themselves* struggle with exactly this: "difficulty understanding callout and leader-line ownership — connecting labels to diagram elements" is called out as a specific, named failure mode, not a solved capability **[confirmed]** ([Enginuity, arXiv:2606.03410](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.03410)). That is direct evidence *against* reaching for a VLM to solve leader-line ownership — a geometric nearest-neighbor/ray-casting approach over the vector data is both cheaper and, per this evidence, likely to outperform a VLM at this specific sub-task. |
| **Symbol recognition (weld symbols)** | **Partially — vector-native approaches exist but are less mature than raster CNN/YOLO pipelines** | AWS A2.4 weld symbols are themselves compositional vector primitives (reference line, arrow, tail, and a small fixed vocabulary of geometric marks placed above/below the line) **[confirmed]** ([Weld Symbol Chart, AWS A2.4](https://clause5.io/welding/symbols/weld-symbol-chart/)). Published deep-learning work on engineering-drawing symbol detection is overwhelmingly raster/bounding-box detector based (YOLO, Faster R-CNN, R-FCN, SSD) rather than vector-graph based, and explicitly cites class imbalance and scarce labeled data as the dominant real obstacle, addressed with GANs for synthetic augmentation **[confirmed]** ([Deep learning for symbols detection and classification in engineering drawings](https://rgu-repository.worktribe.com/preview/928735/ELYAN%202020%20Deep%20learning.pdf); [Few-Shot Symbol Detection in Engineering Drawings](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/08839514.2024.2406712)). A separate, more directly analogous line of work (P&ID digitization) shows both approaches actively coexist: graph-based one-shot symbol recognition on vector/contour-derived graphs exists and works **[confirmed]** ([OSSR-PID, IJCNN 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03849)) but is explicitly research-stage, validated on ~100 synthetic + 12 real sheets only. **This audit found zero weld-symbol occurrences catalogued in the 7-project scan** — this is a real gap in the corpus evidence, flagged here as **not established for this corpus** rather than assumed absent. |
| **Hatch patterns / material fill symbols** | **Mostly yes for regular repeating patterns; edge cases genuinely visual** | PDF fill patterns are frequently encoded as actual repeating vector tile patterns or dash-spaced hatch lines in the content stream, extractable without rasterization; irregular or rasterized-fill hatches (embedded as a bitmap fill) would need pixel decoding. No dedicated recent literature on PDF-native (non-rasterized) hatch classification was found in this research pass — treated as **[inferred]** from general vector-pattern-extraction feasibility, not confirmed by a dedicated study. |
| **Section/detail-boundary recognition** (match lines, detail bubbles) | **Yes** | Detail/section call-out bubbles are near-universally a vector circle/oval/hexagon primitive plus contained text — both are already in the PDF's vector-path and text layers. No VLM needed; this is a shape-classification problem over known vector primitive types, the same category of problem CAD panoptic-symbol-spotting research already addresses at the vector-primitive level **[inferred]**, extending ([CADTransformer](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Fan_CADTransformer_Panoptic_Symbol_Spotting_Transformer_for_CAD_Drawings_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)). |
| **Callout-bubble reading** | **Yes** | Pure text-in-vector-shape extraction; no pixel step required, same reasoning as above. |
| **Graphic legend syntax** (the corpus's own confirmed `W27X84 [88] c=1"` bracket-notation example) | **Yes — this is explicitly a text/grammar problem, not a vision problem** | The audit already confirmed this legend is rendered as ordinary extractable text tokens (`[88]`, `c=1"`) positioned near a section-mark string — the *challenge* is that it's a compact, undocumented mini-grammar, not that the characters are unreadable pixels. This is squarely a document-language/legend-extraction problem (Section L, task 1), not a VLM problem. |
| **Orientation** (north arrows, view-direction indicators) | **Yes** | These are themselves vector arrow primitives (often literally the same arrowhead sub-path construct used for leader lines and dimension lines) with an associated rotation transform already present in the PDF content stream's transformation matrix — orientation is recoverable as a number, not a picture, without rendering anything. |
| **Visually-linked text blocks not adjacent in the PDF text stream** | **Yes, and this is the audit's own explicit finding** | The audit already demonstrated (§F.6, §J) that "adjacency" failures in this corpus are text-*extraction*-stream artifacts (PyMuPDF line-break insertion) and layout-proximity problems, not vision problems — the fix is spatial clustering over word bounding-box coordinates (already extracted), the same signal LayoutLM-family "layout" features use. Document-layout GNN work formalizes exactly this: representing a document as a graph over text/graphic nodes with spatial-proximity edges, built from coordinates alone, no pixels **[confirmed]** ([Doc2Graph, ICDAR 2023 workshop](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11168); [DocGraphLM, SIGIR 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02823)). |

### K.3 Net assessment: where does this corpus's evidence actually point?

**None of the eight problem categories above, as instantiated in this specific 7-PDF corpus, require rasterizing the page.** Every one is, in principle, a text-layer, vector-path-geometry, or layout-coordinate problem. This is a stronger, more specific version of the prior audit's own conclusion (§K/§O of the base audit) that the VLM-per-crop approach targets "the wrong end of the problem."

Two genuine caveats, both **[inferred]**, not confirmed against this corpus (because this corpus doesn't contain the triggering condition):

1. **Scanned/faxed/hand-redlined pages.** All 7 audited PDFs are 100% vector — but a wider deployment corpus plausibly includes scanned as-builts, faxed RFI markups, or hand-redlined field copies. Those pages have no extractable vector geometry or reliable text layer, and *would* be a genuine pixel/OCR problem. This is the actual scenario general-purpose OCR/VLM document pipelines (Donut, LayoutLMv3, GOT-OCR2.0) were built for **[confirmed]** ([Donut / LayoutLMv3 overview](https://www.kungfu.ai/blog-post/engineering-explained-layoutlmv3-and-the-future-of-document-ai)) — but it is a different corpus than the one audited, and no evidence currently exists that Estima3D's actual document intake contains such pages.
2. **Coarse page-role / "does this page read as a detail sheet vs. a schedule sheet" gestalt judgment** (the audit's own proposed `template_detail` status, §I). This could be done from layout statistics alone (text density, box-count histograms — all vector/text-derived), but a page-thumbnail classifier is a legitimate, cheap place a lightweight vision signal could add value without needing full VLM reasoning — this is a borderline case, not a clear "needs pixels" case.

Independent evidence that frontier VLMs are *not* already solving the vision-dependent slice of this problem space well: a 2026 benchmark purpose-built for architecture/engineering drawings found models hit "up to 0.95 accuracy" on OCR/text-centric tasks but only **0.40–0.55 accuracy on symbol-centric understanding** (e.g., reliably counting doors/windows on floor plans), concluding current systems lack "robust drawing literacy" **[confirmed]** ([AECV-Bench, arXiv:2601.04819](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.04819), Kondratenko et al., Jan 2026). A mechanical-drawing-specific benchmark found the same pattern inverted usefully: general VLMs handle **Text & Table reading and Dimension & Annotation recognition well (90–98% for the best fine-tuned model)** but perform far worse on **Item Localization (19–64%)** and multi-view **Projection reasoning (64%)**, with the paper's own diagnosis being that "high annotation density and symbol clutter make decisive cues easy to miss" and "unreliable spatial relation reasoning under strict projection rules" **[confirmed]** ([MechVQA, arXiv:2605.30794](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.30794v1), Kou et al., May 2026). GPT-4o specifically scored only 63.06% overall on MechVQA versus a fine-tuned specialist model's 84.85% **[confirmed]**, ibid. This pattern — VLMs are already good at the *text-reading* sub-problem this corpus mostly needs, and bad at the *symbol/spatial* sub-problem this corpus mostly doesn't expose (because it's vector, not pixel-locked) — reinforces K.2's conclusion rather than contradicting it.

### K.4 A scientifically credible VLM evaluation methodology

The partner's 4-crop pilot had three independent, fixable design flaws, each mapped below to a standard methodology from the CV/NLP literature that exists specifically to fix it.

**Flaw 1 — no unimodal ablation, so "reads BENT PL" and "sees a bend" are indistinguishable.** This is exactly the "language bias" / "shortcut learning" problem that a decade of VQA research has built standard tooling for. The **VQA-CP** benchmark was built specifically to penalize models that answer from question/text priors instead of the image **[confirmed]** ([VQA-CP leaderboard](https://github.com/cdancette/vqa-cp-leaderboard)), and the standard diagnostic is a **question-only (text-only) baseline model** trained/run with the image removed — "an interesting way to quantify unimodal bias... training models using only one of the two modalities... the question-only model is a particularly strong baseline because of the large amount of statistical regularities that can be leveraged from the question modality" **[confirmed]** ([RUBi: Reducing Unimodal Biases in VQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1906.10169)). This is the direct literature precedent for the text-only condition specified below. The general finding that motivates doing this at all: "questions starting with 'Is there a clock' has the answer 'yes' 98% of the time" in the original VQA benchmark — i.e., a text-only model can look deceptively competent on a poorly-designed test **[confirmed]** ([overview of VQA language bias](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10971294/)).

**Flaw 2 — no class-word masking, so the model could be doing literal OCR pattern-matching on "BENT."** The standard technique here is **input erasure / occlusion-based attribution**: mask or erase the candidate word and measure whether the output changes — "occlusion is a black-box attribution method that works by masking each word and looking at the impact on model output" **[confirmed]** ([Local Interpretations for Explainable NLP: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/html/2103.11072v3)); a closely related technique, **input marginalization**, formalizes replacing a token and measuring output shift as an interpretability method in its own right **[confirmed]** ([Interpretation of NLP models through input marginalization](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347236620_Interpretation_of_NLP_models_through_input_marginalization)). Applied here: redact/blur only the literal pixels spelling "BENT" (or "BENT PL") inside the crop while leaving the geometric fold-line intact, and see whether classification survives — this is the single most direct test of the audit's own stated concern (§O) that the pilot's positive results are confounded by the crop literally containing the word.

**Flaw 3 — no hard negatives.** Fine-grained visual classification research is explicit that discriminative power is the thing that collapses under confusable classes even when ordinary detection looks fine: "all detectors have acceptable performance when localizing objects in the absence of confounding labels, but discriminative power in hard-negative settings drops drastically" **[confirmed]** ([The devil is in the fine-grained details: evaluating open-vocabulary detectors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2311.17518)). This corpus already has real, confirmed hard negatives ready to use as-is: `BENTONITE ROPE` (pure substring collision), `BENT GRIPSTAY` (proprietary product name), `HSS16X4X1/2 BENT` (bent section, not a plate) — per the base audit §E, 13 of 125 raw "BENT" occurrences (10.4%) are exactly this kind of confusable, confirmed by direct reading.

**A directly on-domain precedent for the proposed ablation matrix already exists.** A 2026 CAD-blueprint-reasoning benchmark reports separate accuracy numbers for a **"Test Image Only"** condition (80.86%) versus an **"+Attribute Explanation"** condition that adds textual/semantic context alongside the image (84.03%) **[confirmed]** ([CReFT-CAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.10865v1) results as summarized via [project repo](https://github.com/KeNiu042/CReFT-CAD)) — i.e., exactly the image-only vs. image+text split proposed below is not a novel idea invented for this audit; it is becoming a standard reporting convention in CAD/blueprint VLM evaluation specifically.

#### Proposed test matrix

| Condition | What it isolates |
|---|---|
| **Text-only** (no image, extracted text only) | Upper bound of what a pure NLP/regex/LLM-on-text approach already achieves — the RUBi/VQA-CP "question-only" baseline, adapted |
| **Image-only** (crop, no OCR text passed) | Whether the model can classify from geometry/visual-symbol alone, forcing it to actually look |
| **Image + text** | The realistic production condition (image plus the extracted-text context around it) |
| **Masked-class-words image** (crop with "BENT"/"PL"/etc. pixels redacted, rest of geometry intact) | Erasure/occlusion test — isolates whether performance survives when the literal trigger word is unreadable |
| **Image + project-profile** (crop + the document's own Drawing Language Profile entry, per base audit §L) | Whether document-level context (not just local OCR) changes the answer — tests Architecture C's actual value proposition against Architecture B |
| **Geometry-only** (vector path data extracted and passed as structured coordinates/angles, no image, no OCR text) | Tests the K.2 hypothesis directly: can a text-based reasoner given *only* the raw vector geometry (line endpoints/angles) already discriminate bent vs. straight, without any pixel step at all |
| **Geometry + text** | The full non-VLM alternative pipeline — if this matches or beats image-only/image+text, it is direct evidence the VLM adds nothing this corpus needs |

Required test set composition, grounded in the corpus already catalogued: the 8 positive classes specified (BENT_PLATE, PLATE, CAP_PLATE, BASE_PLATE, GUSSET, CONNECTION_PLATE, BENT_HSS, ANGLE) instantiated with **real corpus examples** (the audit's own 112 confirmed BENT PL instances plus its plate-family sibling terms give real seed text for 6 of the 8 classes already), plus the 3 confirmed hard-negative categories (BENTONITE, BENT GRIPSTAY, bent-HSS) as **required, not optional**, test cases — precedent for requiring hard negatives as a first-class part of the test set, not an afterthought, per the fine-grained classification literature above.

**Sample size discipline:** the partner's pilot used n=4. Standard fine-grained/VQA bias-diagnosis work runs on datasets of thousands to tens of thousands of examples (VQA-CP, MechVQA at 20,778 QA pairs, Enginuity at 494+60). The base audit's own §M/§O already flags that the 7-project corpus itself is likely too small to generalize from — any VLM eval here should be sized to at minimum cover every confirmed positive instance (112 BENT PL + 491 plate-family) and every confirmed hard negative (13), not a hand-picked handful, and ideally should be expanded beyond the 7-project corpus before being treated as conclusive **[inferred]** from the base audit's own recommendation (§O.4).

#### Metrics

- **Precision / recall / F1 per class**, not aggregate accuracy — aggregate accuracy on an 8-class-plus-negatives set is dominated by the easy majority class and would hide exactly the confusable-class failures this eval exists to find (standard practice in the fine-grained/hard-negative literature above).
- **False-positive rate specifically on the hard-negative set** (BENTONITE/GRIPSTAY/bent-HSS firing as BENT_PLATE) — reported separately from general FPR, since this is the audit's own named failure mode.
- **Calibration** — Expected Calibration Error (ECE): bin predictions by stated/derived confidence and measure the gap between confidence and empirical accuracy. This matters because LLM/VLM confidence is well documented to be unreliable on its own: "while LLMs can achieve high classification accuracy... their confidence estimates remain severely miscalibrated across all tested formats, with Expected Calibration Errors ranging from 0.108 to 0.427" **[confirmed]** ([Assessing and Mitigating Miscalibration in LLM-Based Measurement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.11954)); "transformer-based language models are often miscalibrated and tend to be overconfident" **[confirmed]** ([Survey of Confidence Estimation and Calibration in LLMs](https://aclanthology.org/2024.naacl-long.366.pdf)). A model that is accurate but overconfident on hard negatives is a worse production risk than one that is accurate and appropriately unsure.
- **Structured-output validity rate** — the fraction of responses that parse as valid JSON against the target schema on the first attempt, without retry. This is now a standard, directly benchmarkable metric: **JSONSchemaBench** exists specifically to measure this across models and constraint-decoding methods **[confirmed]** ([JSONSchemaBench, arXiv:2501.10868](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.10868)); constrained-decoding approaches can reach "near-perfect schema accuracy (99.5%)" while free-form prompting is far less reliable **[confirmed]** ([Structured Output Generation in LLMs](https://medium.com/@emrekaratas-ai/structured-output-generation-in-llms-json-schema-and-grammar-based-decoding-6a5c58b698a6)) — this metric would have caught the partner's reported malformed-JSON problem systematically rather than anecdotally.
- **Latency**, reported per-image and for the full test-set batch, both because the partner already reported "slow inference" **[reported, not verified]** (carried from base audit) and because production throughput at GCDC's 81-page scale depends on it — report against realistic local-serving baselines (e.g., quantized 70B-class throughput figures under vLLM continuous batching) so a latency number is comparable to a known reference point **[confirmed]** ([AWQ/GGUF quantization and vLLM throughput](https://www.spheron.network/blog/awq-quantization-guide-llm-deployment/)).

### K.5 Bottom line for K

Per-problem, vector/text pipelines cover essentially everything this specific 7-PDF corpus contains, including the two areas (leader-arrow ownership, bent-vs-straight geometry) most likely to look "visual" at first glance — and the external benchmark evidence (AECV-Bench, MechVQA, Enginuity) shows current frontier VLMs are *already weak* on exactly the symbol/spatial sub-problems this corpus's vector-native format sidesteps, while being strong on the text-reading sub-problem this corpus's vector-native format already gives away for free. The only genuinely pixel-dependent scenario identified is content this specific corpus does not contain (scanned/faxed/hand-redlined pages) — real for a wider deployment, but **not established** as present in Estima3D's actual document intake today.

### L.3 Vector-native alternatives to raster VLMs

Since the corpus is 100% born-digital vector — not scanned — the more interesting research question is whether there's a mature alternative to rasterizing at all.

| System / approach | What it does | Domain match to structural-steel PDFs | Maturity |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Doc2Graph / DocGraphLM** | Represents a document as a graph over text/graphic nodes with spatial-proximity edges (built from coordinates, no pixels); GNN or graph-augmented language model reasons over that graph for key-information-extraction, form understanding, table detection **[confirmed]** ([Doc2Graph](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.11168); [DocGraphLM](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.02823)) | **Good match for the legend/notes-page and layout-clustering sub-problems** (K.2's "visually-linked text blocks" row) — this is essentially a formalized version of what the base audit's own proposed layout-proximity fix would need | Closest to production-usable of everything surveyed here — evaluated on standard KIE benchmarks (forms, invoices), with a published multilingual extension (Doc2Graph-X) **[confirmed]** ([Doc2Graph-X](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-3-031-94139-9_24)); worth prototyping directly against the base audit's page-role/legend-classification sub-problem |
| **CADTransformer / ArchCAD-400K** | Panoptic symbol spotting over CAD vector primitives (windows, doors, walls) for architectural floor plans; newer ArchCAD-400K dataset is "26 times larger than previous datasets" with a Dual-Pathway Symbol Spotter combining image and point-cloud features **[confirmed]** ([ArchCAD-400K](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.22346)) | **Domain-adjacent, not domain-matched** — built for architectural floor-plan symbols (doors/windows/furniture), not structural-steel plate/connection callouts; the underlying technique (sample vector-primitive features, classify at the entity level) generalizes conceptually but no structural-steel-specific instantiation exists in the literature found here | Research-stage but relatively mature within its own (architectural floor-plan) niche — has a large purpose-built dataset and multiple published follow-ons, unlike the P&ID work below |
| **OSSR-PID (one-shot symbol recognition, P&ID)** | Samples pixels along vector-contour boundaries into paths, builds a graph, classifies with a DGCNN — explicitly graph-based, not raster-CNN **[confirmed]** ([OSSR-PID](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.03849)) | **Closer domain match** — P&ID (piping/instrumentation) symbol-and-connectivity digitization is a closer industrial analog to structural-steel plate/connection-callout digitization than architectural floor plans are | **Research-stage, small-scale**: validated on ~100 synthetic diagrams plus a 12-sheet private real dataset — an order of magnitude below production-scale validation |
| **P&ID-to-graph via transformer (Relationformer)** | Joint end-to-end extraction of symbols *and* their interconnections into a structured graph in one pass, rather than separate symbol-detection + line-detection stages **[confirmed]** ([From Engineering Diagrams to Graphs, DSAA 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2411.13929)) | Directly relevant conceptually — connectivity-aware digitization is exactly what a "which leader line points at which plate" problem needs — but note this specific system **operates on raster images**, not vector data, despite being architecturally a graph-output transformer | Research-stage: introduces "the first publicly accessible benchmark dataset for P&ID digitization," i.e. the field didn't even have a shared benchmark before this paper |
| **SketchGraphs / Vitruvion / BRepNet** | Graph/constraint-based generative and representation-learning models for **parametric CAD sketches** (2D sketch primitives + designer-imposed geometric constraints) and **B-rep** 3D solid models **[confirmed]** ([SketchGraphs](https://github.com/PrincetonLIPS/SketchGraphs); [Vitruvion, ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2109.14124)) | **Domain mismatch** — these operate on native CAD authoring data (parametric constraint graphs, B-rep solids) generated *inside* a CAD tool, not on flattened construction-PDF vector paths that have already lost parametric/constraint information at PDF-export time. Not directly applicable without a CAD-source (not PDF-derived) pipeline | Mature *within mechanical CAD generative modeling* research, but the wrong input representation for this corpus |
| **DeepSVG / UniSVG** | Hierarchical transformer over SVG path-command sequences for vector-graphic generation/representation learning **[confirmed]** ([DeepSVG](https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.11301); [UniSVG](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07766)) | Architecturally relevant precedent (transformer directly over vector path commands, no rasterization) but built for icon-scale generative SVG tasks, not page-scale technical-drawing interpretation | Mature as a generative-modeling technique; not demonstrated on anything resembling a construction drawing |

**Bottom line for L.3:** no vector-native alternative surveyed here is mature enough to be a drop-in production replacement for either the document-language-extraction task or the symbol/geometry task. The one closest to production-usable today is **Doc2Graph/DocGraphLM-style document-graph modeling**, and specifically for the layout/legend-page-role sub-problem this repo already needs (base audit §I/§J) — that is well-trodden KIE-benchmark territory, not a research bet. The graph/vector approaches aimed at actual engineering-symbol and connectivity recognition (CADTransformer/ArchCAD, OSSR-PID, the P&ID Relationformer work) are all explicitly research-stage, validated on small or narrowly-scoped datasets, and built for adjacent-but-different domains (architectural floor plans, piping diagrams, parametric CAD authoring) rather than structural-steel construction PDFs specifically — worth a small, cheap prototype spike if the K.3 borderline cases (page-role gestalt, weld-symbol/hatch edge cases) turn out to matter in practice, but not something to build a production bet on today.

## Summary judgment (K + L combined)

The corpus-specific evidence in K.2–K.3, cross-checked against three independent 2026 benchmarks built specifically to test VLMs on technical/engineering drawings (AECV-Bench, MechVQA, Enginuity), converges on the same point the base audit already reached from a different angle: **this corpus's actual failure modes are not vision problems.** The two things that look most "visual" at first glance — leader-arrow ownership and bend geometry — are exactly the two things (a) recoverable from vector-path data already in hand, and (b) independently confirmed as current VLM weak points, not strengths, by external benchmarks. Where a VLM prototype is justified, it is narrow and specific (K.3's genuinely pixel-dependent scenarios, none confirmed present in this corpus today) rather than a general "add a VLM stage." If and when that prototype is built, **Qwen2.5-VL/Qwen3-VL is the best-evidenced candidate** for diagram-specific quality plus a genuinely open license plus native high-resolution tiling, with **GLM-4.6V** as a second open-weight option worth a side-by-side test.

## M. Controlled experiments

The guiding principle, consistent with standard "start without ML" engineering practice — see Google's *Rules of Machine Learning*, Rule #1 ("Don't be afraid to launch a product without machine learning" — a heuristic gets you partway to where ML would, at a fraction of the cost) and Rule #4 ("keep the first model simple and get the infrastructure right") [[Rules of Machine Learning](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/guides/rules-of-ml)] — is: **exhaust cheap, deterministic, fully-explainable tests against the corpus that already exists before spending anything on model training or per-crop VLM calls.** This mirrors the "minimum viable experiment" framing in product/PoC methodology — the smallest test that can falsify a specific assumption, using existing artifacts rather than new infrastructure [[Minimum Viable Experiment (MVE)](https://www.ideaplan.io/glossary/minimum-viable-experiment-mve)] [[AI Proof of Concept (PoC) Guide](https://smartdev.com/the-ultimate-guide-to-ai-proof-of-concept-poc-from-strategy-to-implementation/)]. None of the experiments below require new data acquisition, model training, or fine-tuning; all run against the existing 7-project corpus and its already-built artifacts (`scan_legend_pages.py`, `scan_summary.json`, `hard_case_corpus.json`, all in the audit's folder).

Priority order — cheapest and most conclusive first:

### 1. Variable-arity plate grammar fix + full-corpus regression (deterministic, no ML)
**Tests:** whether loosening `_PLATE`'s dimension-arity requirement (`{1,3}` → `{0,3}` with a plate-family role-word co-requirement when the dimension count is 0, per `engineering_object_filter.py:14-17`) and adding a `fabrication_modifier` field recovers the confirmed losses without new false positives.
**Data:** the existing 7-project corpus; specifically the 491 plate-family hits and 112 confirmed bent-plate occurrences already catalogued (`§E`), plus the 13 confirmed hard negatives (`BENTONITE`, `BENT GRIPSTAY`, bent-HSS) and the 12 strict legend pages (`§B`) as the false-positive check set.
**Pass:** recovery rate on the known 112 bent-plate + 379 sibling plate-family (491−112) instances rises sharply (the audit's own directly-executed sample found 5 of 6 real callouts failing today, `§H`) while the false-positive rate on the 13 hard negatives and 12 legend pages does not increase from its current (effectively zero, by omission) baseline.
**Fail:** recovery improves but false positives rise materially (e.g. the fix starts firing on `BENT GRIPSTAY` or `BENTONITE` because the role-word check is too loose) — signals the role-word co-requirement needs tightening (adjacency window, not just line presence) before promotion.

### 2. Page-role locator as a standalone deterministic classifier
**Tests:** whether a page-level "legend/general-notes vs. typical-detail vs. framing-detail" signal (coarse 2–3-way split per `§L`'s `page_roles`, not the finer four/five-way split) can be produced reliably from the same keyword-density scoring already prototyped in `scan_legend_pages.py` (`§B`), and whether it closes the LGD-002 gap (`§H`) without breaking LGD-001.
**Data:** the 12 known strict legend pages, the 92 "loose" pages, and a hand-labeled sample (~30–50 pages) of typical-detail and framing-detail pages across the 7 projects for a role-classification ground truth that doesn't exist yet.
**Pass:** the classifier correctly separates the 12 known legend pages from a stratified sample of non-legend pages, and specifically flips the LGD-002 case (`"W8"=W8x10` inside general notes, not currently suppressed) to "on a legend/notes page" while leaving LGD-001 (correctly suppressed today) unchanged.
**Fail:** page-role signal is too coarse to distinguish "legend page containing an illustrative example to suppress" from "legend page containing a declared project rule to capture" (GCDC's own abbreviation table is itself on a legend page and must *not* be suppressed) — this is a real edge case the experiment should surface, not assume away.

### 3. Document-scoped LLM legend-profile extraction, validated against actual callouts — GCDC only
**Tests:** the specific "LLM-as-legend-parser-with-validation" idea (`§L`, `§M` of the audit) on the single project with the richest evidence. Feed GCDC page 5's *text* (not an image) to an LLM constrained to the `§L` schema, then **programmatically re-scan the other 80 pages for literal occurrences of `"W8"`, `"W10"`, `"C8"`, `"C12"`, `"HSS8x4"`** to determine whether the substitution table is actually used elsewhere in the document (flagged `requires_engineer_review` and never checked in the audit itself).
**Data:** GCDC's 81-page PDF; the 8 known substitution pairs as extraction ground truth (`§D`); the rescan is a simple deterministic regex pass, not an LLM call.
**Pass:** the LLM correctly extracts all 8 pairs with no invented ones (precision and recall both measurable against known ground truth), *and* the rescan produces a factual, citable answer — either "found N bare `W8`-family callouts elsewhere, consistent with the rule" or "found none — the rule is unused boilerplate on this document" — either outcome is a valid, informative pass, because this experiment is testing the validation mechanism, not hoping for a particular answer. This mirrors the "reconstruction-as-validation" principle from recent document-processing literature: a validator should always check the extraction against the *original source*, never against the extraction's own claim, to avoid circular confirmation [[RaV-IDP: A Reconstruction-as-Validation Framework for Faithful Intelligent Document Processing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23644)].
**Fail:** the LLM hallucinates substitution pairs not present in the text, or the rescan can't be run deterministically (e.g. because `"W8"` also appears in unrelated contexts requiring disambiguation) — either result would argue for tightening the schema/prompt or reconsidering how "validated" is defined before this pattern is generalized to all 7 projects.

### 4. Detail-reference graph micro-POC on one project
**Tests:** whether a plan-sheet callout bubble (e.g. "SEE 4/S301") can be reliably matched to its corresponding detail sheet using text-pattern matching over the existing vector text layer (no geometry/leader-line detection needed for a first pass), which is the mechanism the audit's `template_detail` finding depends on (`§I`, `§G`: essentially all confirmed bent-plate evidence lives on detail sheets referenced by callout bubble, never dimensioned directly on plan sheets).
**Data:** one project with a clean plan/detail split — Burrville (29 pp.) is a reasonable first choice given its confirmed BENT PL presence across multiple sheets (`§E`). Requires a small amount of new manual ground truth: enumerate every plan-sheet callout-bubble reference and its corresponding detail-sheet occurrence by hand (this is bounded — tens, not hundreds, of references on a 29-page set).
**Pass:** a simple regex/string-matching pass (e.g. detail-number and sheet-number co-occurrence) recovers a majority of the hand-verified reference pairs at high precision (a wrong link corrupts a quantity; a missed link merely under-counts, so precision should be weighted higher than recall here — see `§S`).
**Fail:** callout bubbles are graphical (drawn as a leader line + circle, not consistently rendered as parseable text in the PDF text layer) and text-pattern matching alone can't recover most references — this would be a specific, useful negative result establishing that the detail-reference graph needs geometry/vector-drawing analysis, not just text matching, before further investment.

### 5. TEMPLATE_DETAIL page-role classification POC
**Tests:** whether a simple heuristic (dimensioned content + no grid/plan-location reference + presence of a detail-number title block) can distinguish `template_detail` pages from `framing_detail` (plan-view, located) pages — the third bucket the audit's evidence argues for (`§I`) but which the brief's original four-way framing didn't anticipate.
**Data:** the same ~30–50 hand-labeled page sample from Experiment 2, re-labeled with the finer template_detail/framing_detail split.
**Pass:** heuristic achieves reasonable separation on the hand-labeled sample; disagreements are inspected to see if they cluster around a fixable rule (e.g. presence of a scale bar or "TYP." marking) rather than being unpredictable.
**Fail:** the distinction turns out to require genuine layout/visual judgment (e.g. distinguishing a "typical detail with no location" from a "framing detail tied to a specific grid line" by drawing style alone) — this is exactly the kind of residual case that would justify Experiment 7 (selective VLM), not before.

### 6. Generalize the legend-profile extraction (Experiment 3) to all 7 projects
**Tests:** whether the GCDC POC's precision/recall holds up across projects with different note styles (only GCDC has the member-size substitution table; the other 6 projects test the profile extractor's ability to correctly extract *less* — connection vocabulary, bent-plate documentation, interchangeable-term notes — without over-generating rules that don't exist).
**Data:** all 7 projects; ground truth is largely already built in the audit itself (`§C`, `§D`, `§E`, `§G`).
**Pass:** extraction precision/recall stays comparable to the GCDC POC across projects; specifically, the extractor should *not* invent a member-size substitution table on the 6 projects that don't have one (a negative-case check).
**Fail:** performance degrades sharply outside GCDC's clean numbered-note format — informs whether prompt/schema needs per-firm tuning or whether a more robust legend-page locator (Experiment 2) needs to feed it more precisely-scoped text.

### 7. Selective VLM comparison — last, and only on the residual
**Tests:** per the audit's own `§O` design (not the partner's original 4-crop pilot): each case run image-only, image-with-class-word-masked, image+extracted-text, and image+text+profile — explicitly to separate "reads the word BENT" from "sees a bend" from "knows this project's vocabulary." Required cases include the confirmed hard negatives (`BENTONITE`, `BENT GRIPSTAY`, bent-HSS) and the LGD-002 abbreviation-table case as a required negative.
**Data:** the `hard_case_corpus.json` seed set (`§O` of the audit) plus whatever residual, genuinely-visual cases survive Experiments 1–6 (most plausibly `template_detail` classification, if Experiment 5 fails).
**Pass:** VLM beats a text-only baseline specifically on the *masked-text* subset (proving geometric/visual understanding, not OCR) — see `§S` for the numeric gate.
**Fail:** VLM performance collapses when the class word is masked, confirming the audit's suspicion (`§O`) that the partner's earlier positive results were confounded by literal text presence in the crop, not genuine visual understanding — consistent with recent findings that VLMs can solve benchmarks via language/OCR shortcuts rather than real visual reasoning [[TopoPerception: A Shortcut-Free Evaluation of Global Visual Perception in Large Vision-Language Models](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11831)] [[VISTA-Bench: Do Vision-Language Models Really Understand Visualized Text as Well as Pure Text?](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04802)].

**Explicitly not proposed:** any model fine-tuning run, any new large-scale data collection, or any multi-week training effort before Experiments 1–6 are complete and their results have identified a genuine residual gap that only a learned model (contextual ranker or VLM) could plausibly close. Experiments 1–2 are pure code changes and page-scoring changes; Experiments 3–6 are LLM *calls* used as a one-shot parser/proposal-generator, not a trained model; only Experiment 7, gated behind all of the above, touches anything resembling a model comparison, and even that is inference-only against an off-the-shelf VLM, not training.

## N. Benchmark

**Design principle:** a single overall accuracy number would average together failures that need completely different fixes (a candidate-generation miss needs a grammar fix; a modifier-drop needs token-grouping logic; a page-role miss needs a document-level signal) — this is the same reasoning behind staged/decoupled evaluation in recent document-AI benchmark design, where downstream stages are evaluated only on correctly-resolved upstream outputs specifically so that an early-stage error (e.g. wrong page localized) doesn't get blamed on, or mask, a later-stage metric (e.g. fact extraction) [[CiteVQA: Benchmarking Evidence Attribution for Trustworthy Document Intelligence](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12882v1)]. The same "evaluate each stage on its own dataset and metric rather than inferring component quality from an end-to-end score" principle is echoed generally in ML pipeline evaluation practice, where an end-to-end benchmark can mask a broken component that happens to be compensated for elsewhere in the pipeline [[Dataset Evaluation Framework](https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/dataset-evaluation-framework)]. Field-level exact-match/F1 scoring (treating a field as wrong even on a one-character miss) is standard in document-AI evaluation tooling and is a reasonable default for stages 3–5 below [[Best Document AI Evaluation Tools](https://www.extend.ai/resources/best-evaluation-benchmarking-tools-document-ai)]. Where fields are *interdependent* (a section token and its modifier), tuple-level (joint) evaluation rather than independent per-field averaging is the correct approach — this distinction is drawn explicitly in the multi-stage extraction literature and applies directly to Stage 4 below [[Reliable End-to-End Material Information Extraction... with Source-Tracked Multi-Stage LLMs](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05142)]. A recent evaluation of LLM/VLM document extraction in a genuinely high-risk domain (student transcripts, comparable in stakes to a cost-estimating pipeline feeding real bids) is a useful methodological analog: it used F1/precision/recall with strict field normalization and found the large majority of off-the-shelf configurations scored below F1 = 0.25 without domain-specific tuning — a caution against assuming an LLM-heavy approach will "just work" on structured extraction without staged, adversarial evaluation [[Evaluating Structured Information Extraction with Open Models in a High Risk Public Sector Application](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.18289)] **[external finding, different domain — cited as a methodology analog, not a direct prediction for this corpus]**.

Every stage below should be tested against the audit's existing evidence (counts, quotes, hard-negative set) as its seed ground truth, and every stage's test set must deliberately include the confirmed adversarial cases (`BENTONITE`, `BENT GRIPSTAY`, bent-HSS, the LGD-002 abbreviation-table false negative, the GCDC allowed/prohibited contradiction) rather than relying only on random held-out sampling.

| Stage | What it measures | Proposed metric | Ground truth source (this corpus) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Candidate recovery** | Did we stop silently dropping valid objects before classification even runs? | **Candidate Recall** = (# real objects surviving to candidate stage) / (# real objects manually confirmed present in raw text). Computed separately for plate-family and non-plate-family tokens. | The 491 plate-family + 112 bent-plate confirmed occurrences (`§E`), plus a similarly-scanned steel-section baseline for comparison. |
| **2. Object classification** | Plate vs. bent plate vs. cap plate vs. base plate vs. conn plate vs. gusset vs. stiffener vs. clip angle. | **Macro-F1 across classes**, plus a full confusion matrix (not just the aggregate) — near-miss confusions between sibling plate types are exactly the failure mode worth seeing explicitly, not averaging away. | `§D`'s term-family table; `hard_case_corpus.json`. |
| **3. Dimension extraction** | Thickness/width/length correctly captured, including correctly recognizing when a dimension is legitimately absent (thickness-only convention) vs. wrongly missing. | **Field-level exact-match accuracy** per dimension slot, with an explicit three-way outcome (correct / correctly-absent / wrongly-missing-or-wrong) rather than binary — collapsing "thickness-only by convention" into "incomplete" would misrepresent ~most real plate callouts in this corpus (`§E` pattern 2). | The field-verified variant table in `§E` (11 real field-callout variants with citations). |
| **4. Modifier preservation** | `HSS16X4X1/2 BENT` must retain **both** the base section and the modifier — this is a joint condition, not two independent fields. | **Modifier-Preserving Accuracy** = fraction of modifier-bearing objects where base type AND modifier are both correct (an AND, not an average of two F1 scores) — per the tuple/joint-evaluation principle above. | The 2 confirmed bent-HSS instances (`§F.2`) plus the bent-plate-as-modifier cases generally. |
| **5. Project-rule application** | e.g. `W8` → `W8X10` resolved correctly on GCDC, and — critically — **not** over-applied on the 6 projects that don't have this rule. | **Rule Application Precision/Recall**, computed with both a positive corpus (GCDC's 8 pairs, `§D`) and a negative corpus (the other 6 projects, checking the system doesn't invent or cross-apply a rule it only saw once). | `§D`, plus the rescan result from Experiment M-3. |
| **6. Page-role suppression** | Legend/illustrative examples correctly excluded from takeoff, **without** suppressing genuine declared rules that happen to live on the same page. | **Suppression Precision/Recall** using the LGD-001 (should suppress) / LGD-002 (should *not* suppress, `§H`) pair as mandatory test cases, extended to the full 12-page strict legend set plus a stratified non-legend sample. | `§H`, `§I`, `§B`. |
| **7. Detail linking** | Plan reference → detail sheet → requirement correctly recovered. | **Edge Precision/Recall** on the plan-callout → detail-sheet graph, evaluated against hand-built ground truth (Experiment M-4); precision weighted more heavily than recall since a wrong link corrupts a quantity while a missed link only under-counts. | New hand-labeled ground truth (does not yet exist — this is the one stage requiring new manual annotation). |
| **8. Quantity** | One typical detail referenced 5 times produces 5 candidates — not 1 (dedup collapse) or 0 (link failure). | **Multiplicity Accuracy** = rate at which `\|predicted_count − true_count\| = 0`, evaluated *only* on cases where Stage 7 correctly resolved the link (inter-stage decoupling — a Stage-7 failure should show up as a Stage-7 miss, not get blamed on Stage 8's counting logic) [[CiteVQA](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.12882v1)]. | Same ground truth as Stage 7, with reference-count labels added. |
| **9. Human review routing** | Uncertain cases are correctly flagged for review — this is a triage/calibration problem, not an accuracy problem. | **Routing Recall** (of cases that genuinely needed review — missing dimension, contractor-deferred, conflicting notes, unvalidated project rule — what fraction got flagged) and **Routing Precision** (of flagged cases, what fraction actually needed review), evaluated separately because the two failure directions have very different costs (see `§S`). | The six uncertainty categories defined in `§O` below, applied against the full corpus. |

**Reporting format:** a scorecard/matrix (one row per stage, metric + pass/fail against its own gate from `§S`), never a single blended number. Where a stage's ground truth doesn't exist yet in this corpus (Stages 7–9 principally), that is itself reported as a known gap rather than silently substituted with a proxy metric.

## O. Human review workflow

### What a human reviewer needs to see, per uncertainty category

The one design constraint that should hold across every category: **every flagged item shows the exact source quote and page citation that produced it**, because every finding in the underlying audit was itself produced exactly that way (quote + page number, `§D`–`§G`) — it cost nothing extra to produce evidence this way, and it is exactly what HITL design literature identifies as the difference between a reviewer making an informed judgment and a reviewer rubber-stamping a black box: "every human-in-the-loop item needs its source alongside it... without evidence the reviewer is guessing, and a guess recorded as an approval is worse than no control because it creates a false audit trail" [[Human-in-the-Loop Workflows for AI](https://velt.dev/blog/designing-human-in-the-loop-workflows-ai-products)]. Amazon's A2I framework similarly surfaces original document context, extracted value, confidence, and alternative predictions together rather than the extracted value alone [[What is Human-in-the-Loop (HITL)?](https://www.databricks.com/blog/human-in-the-loop)].

**1. Project-specific rule detected but not yet validated** (e.g., GCDC's `"W8"=W8x10` before the cross-document rescan of Experiment M-3 confirms real usage):
> *"I interpreted W8 as W8X10 because General Note 6 on page 5 defines that substitution. I found 3 other bare 'W8' callouts elsewhere in this document (pages 22, 31, 58) that are consistent with this rule — but this has not been confirmed against the actual framing intent. [View note] [Confirm] [Reject] [Flag for engineer review]"*
> — or, if the rescan found zero matches: *"...I found 0 other 'W8' callouts elsewhere in this document. This rule may be unused boilerplate on this project, or the only instance may be one I haven't captured yet. Recommend engineer confirmation before applying."*

**2. Fabricated object exists but is missing a dimension:**
> *"3/8" BENT PL identifies a bent plate thickness, but width and length are not specified — detail geometry / shop design is required. [Scale from drawing] [Enter estimated dimension with source] [Leave undimensioned — quantity to be driven by detail-reference count instead]"* (this last option ties directly to the `template_detail` handling — Stage 7/8 of `§N`).

**3. Contractor-designed/deferred connection:**
> *"This connection (GUSSET, sheet S-401) is explicitly deferred to contractor design per General Note 12: 'GUSSET AND BENT PL TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR.' No takeoff quantity can be derived directly from this drawing set. Recommend routing to allowance-based estimating rather than direct measurement."* — see below for how the AISC delegated-design framework should shape this category specifically.

**4. Conflicting project notes:**
> *"Page 5 lists 'BENT PLATE SHEAR CONNECTIONS' as an allowed connection type (design-table submittal required), and separately states 'BENT PLATE CONNECTIONS WELDED TO THE SUPPORTING MEMBER' are prohibited, on the same page. This may describe a real distinction (bolted allowed / welded prohibited) rather than a contradiction. The callout on page 47 does not specify a fastening method — please confirm which condition applies."*

**5. Ambiguous role/fabrication modifier:**
> *"This plate's fabrication method is given as either bent OR shop-fabricated ('PL 1/4" MIN (BENT OR FABRICATED)') — these are meaningfully different unit costs. Flagging as an estimator choice rather than resolving automatically."*

**6. LLM-derived rule not yet supported by real callouts elsewhere in the document:**
> *"I extracted a possible rule from the general notes ('CANT' = cantilevered beam, page 5) but have not yet found a matching bare 'CANT' callout elsewhere in this document to confirm it is actually used. Treating this as an unvalidated hypothesis, not an applied rule."*
This category should never silently graduate to "applied" — the same bootstrap-constraint idea used in reconstruction-as-validation document processing (always validate against the *original source*, never against the extraction's own output, to avoid circular self-confirmation) applies directly here [[RaV-IDP](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.23644)].

### How professional estimators actually handle these situations today (external research — practitioner-sourced, treat as lower-confidence than the audit's own directly-measured findings; mostly steel-takeoff-vendor and trade-publication sources, not peer-reviewed)

**Typical details and repeated references.** Estimator guidance describes maintaining a "repeat item log" for anything appearing more than roughly ten times on the drawings, specifically because repeated elements (fixtures, connections, reinforcement, duct runs) are the most common source of costly quantity errors and the cheapest to catch early by rechecking the log before pricing [[Construction Takeoff Guide](https://www.estimatingedge.com/the-estimators-ultimate-guide-to-construction-takeoff/)]. Estimators record detail references explicitly by drawing number, detail number, and grid/location reference as part of the takeoff record — i.e., the human process already treats "which detail, referenced from where" as a first-class piece of the takeoff record, not an afterthought [[Material Takeoff: What Is It & Why It Is Essential in Construction](https://www.constructconnect.com/blog/material-takeoff)]. This is direct external support for the audit's `template_detail`/detail-reference-graph proposal (`§I`, `§L`) — it mirrors, rather than invents, an existing manual practice.

**Detail callout bubbles and cross-referencing** are a standardized drafting convention: the bubble/fraction symbol's numerator identifies the detail number, the denominator the sheet it's drawn on, connected by a leader line back to the plan location that invokes it [[Cross Referencing Drawings](https://www5.ebmud.com/specsinternet/projects/01%20Drafting%20Guidelines/FacHelp/Cross_Reference.htm)]. Some newer commercial tools already build an automated "reference graph" across a drawing set specifically to catch broken links [[Annotation & Callout Detection for Construction Drawings](https://helonic.com/features/annotation-detection)] — external validation that a detail-reference graph is a recognized, buildable artifact, not a novel invention for this project.

**Undimensioned / contractor-deferred connections.** This maps closely onto a formal, codified industry framework this audit did not originally reference: **ANSI/AISC 303-22 §3.2.3** requires every connection in a design to be assigned one of three explicit options — *Option 1*: the complete connection design is shown in the contract documents (directly takeoff-able); *Option 2*: the connection is selected or completed by the steel detailer from reference tables the engineer provides (semi-determinate — needs the referenced table, not fully open); *Option 3*: the connection is designed by a licensed engineer working for the fabricator, with substantiating design information submitted back for the EOR's confirmation (genuinely open at drawing-review time) [[Delegating Connection Design — Modern Steel Construction](https://lsc-pagepro.mydigitalpublication.com/article/Delegating+Connection+Design/3624311/653320/article.html)] [[Delegated Connection Design — AISC](https://learning.aisc.org/local/catalog/view/product.php?productid=2645)]. The audit's real contractor-deferred quotes (`"GUSSET AND BENT PL TO BE DESIGNED BY CONTR"`, `§F.4`) are Option-3 language in this framework. **[reasoned proposal]** The review-workflow schema should tag deferred elements with the specific AISC option where the general notes state it explicitly (several of the 7 projects' general notes already discuss connection-design responsibility, `§C` item 3), because this maps a real, already-drafted distinction onto the "flag vs. estimate an allowance" decision rather than inventing a new taxonomy — Option 1 → direct takeoff, Option 2 → needs the referenced table before it's takeoff-able, Option 3 → allowance/fabricator-quote territory, never a direct AI-derived quantity.

**Allowances.** For Option-3-style deferred elements, external estimating guidance repeatedly describes handling incompletely-specified scope as an allowance or unit-price line rather than a computed quantity [[Structural Steel Estimating Services](https://doestimating.com/sub-contractors/structural-steel-estimating-services/)] — consistent with the review-UI recommendation above to route contractor-deferred connections to "allowance-based estimating," not silent omission or a fabricated quantity.

**Pour stops / closure angles / bent plates.** Vendor-published takeoff guidance for deck-edge conditions measures a continuous edge angle by linear footage times weight per foot, not as a discrete count [[Structural Steel Estimating Services — Metal Takeoffs](https://www.structuralsteelestimators.com/)]. This is a meaningfully different unit-of-measure than "count of dimensioned plates," and it directly informs `§T` question 3 below (whether "N references → N candidates" is even the right measurement model for this element family — a continuous edge condition may be measured in LF of edge, not discrete detail-reference count, regardless of how many times a detail bubble happens to be drawn).

**Studs and camber.** Shear studs are described as counted per beam at a given spacing, then multiplied by unit weight for material takeoff [[Metals Estimating Services](https://worldestimating.com/metals-estimating-services/)]; camber appears in the sourced material primarily as a beam-schedule/revision-tracking attribute (flagging which beams changed profile/stud-count/camber between drawing revisions) rather than as an independently priced line item [[Structural Steel Estimating Services](https://worldestimating.com/metals-estimating-services/)]. **[practitioner-sourced, low confidence — this specific point (camber not separately priced) is not strongly corroborated across sources and should be treated as a hypothesis pending direct estimator confirmation, not a settled finding — see `§T` question 7.]**

**Honest caveat on this whole subsection:** the estimating-practice sources found here are overwhelmingly marketing copy from steel-takeoff-service vendors and general construction-estimating guides, not peer-reviewed or standards-body material (the one exception is the AISC 303-22 delegated-design framework, which is a real published standard). They are directionally useful and independently corroborate several of the audit's own architectural instincts (detail-reference tracking as a first-class practice; edge conditions measured by length not count; deferred design routed to allowances) — but they should not be treated as authoritative on any numeric threshold, and the single highest-value next step for this section specifically is a real interview with a structural estimator against this exact 7-project corpus, not further web research (this is elevated to `§T`).

## P. Architecture comparison matrix

**[reasoned proposal — synthesis across the audit's findings, not a benchmarked comparison]**

- **(A) Global deterministic grammar only** — the current production system.
- **(B) Better deterministic grammar (variable-arity) + page-role classification** — Experiments M-1 and M-2, promoted to production.
- **(C) B + a per-project Drawing Language Profile** extracted once (LLM-as-parser) and validated against the document's own callouts before being trusted — Experiments M-3/M-6.
- **(D) C + an ML contextual ranker** using geometry/neighboring-text/page-role/project-rule/relationship features — a genuinely learned model, trained on labels this corpus does not yet have at scale.
- **(E) D + selective VLM fallback**, invoked only for cases the audit's own evidence suggests need pixel/layout understanding (residual `template_detail` visual judgment) — Experiment M-7, gated.

## Q. Recommended architecture

**Recommendation: Architecture C — variable-arity deterministic grammar + page-role classification, with a per-project Drawing Language Profile produced by a document-scoped LLM pass used strictly as a proposal generator, validated against the same document's actual callouts before any rule is trusted.**

This is not a hedge toward "do the simplest thing" for its own sake — it is the architecture the audit's own evidence points at directly, on four separate grounds:

**1. The regex-arity root cause (`§H`) is not an AI problem, and Architecture C fixes it as a code change, not an ML job.** Five of six real, confirmed bent-plate callouts fail today for one precise, mechanical reason: `_PLATE`'s `{1,3}` quantifier requires at least one dimension pair beyond thickness, and the corpus shows the great majority of real plate-family callouts (`§E` pattern 2) give thickness only. This single fix, independently confirmed to generalize across BASE PL/CAP PL/CONN PL/STIFF PL/GUSSET (`§F.1`), plausibly recovers most of the measured loss before any document-level intelligence is added at all. Any architecture — B through F — needs this fix; it is not an argument *for* the more complex options, and building D, E, or F without it first would be paying for machine-learning sophistication to compensate for a bug that a five-line regex change already fixes.

**2. The 3–6% legend-page-vs-detail-sheet split (`§I`) tells you where the *document-level* signal actually needs to operate, and it's small and specific.** Only 3.1% of plate-family hits and 5.6% of BENT hits occur on dedicated legend/cover sheets at all — the overwhelming majority of the real problem (thickness-only plate grammar, detail-sheet content) is already addressed by B. The remaining, genuinely document-scoped problem — distinguishing an illustrative legend example (`W27X84` in a beam-legend key, correctly suppressed today) from a declared project rule sitting on the same kind of page (GCDC's abbreviation table, not suppressed today, `§H`'s LGD-001/LGD-002 pair) — is exactly the narrow, bounded problem a page-role signal plus a document-scoped profile solves, and it is a small enough problem that it doesn't justify a learned ranker (D) or a VLM (E) to solve.

**3. The single-project abbreviation-table finding (`§D`) is real, valuable, and generalizes precisely to what C is designed to capture — but it is one confirmed instance out of seven projects, which argues for a lightweight, cheap-per-document mechanism (one LLM call per document, amortized, `§K`) rather than a trained model that needs many more labeled examples of this pattern than currently exist. Architecture D would need this exact pattern (and others like it) at a scale this 7-project corpus cannot supply; Architecture C needs only one example to justify building the mechanism, because the mechanism is "read the note, then check the note's claim against the rest of the document" — a validation procedure, not a learned generalization.**

**4. The unverified/confounded VLM pilot (`§K`, `§O` of the audit) is specific, sharp evidence against jumping to E or F next.** The partner's earlier positive VLM results are confounded by the crops literally containing the class word — this audit's own hard-negative set (`BENTONITE`, `BENT GRIPSTAY`, bent-HSS) is exactly the kind of confusable case that design never tested, and recent published findings on VLM shortcut learning independently corroborate that this confound is a known, common failure mode of VLM evaluation generally, not a one-off worry [[TopoPerception](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.11831)] [[VISTA-Bench](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04802)]. There is no evidence in this corpus that a genuinely visual/pixel-level understanding problem exists at meaningful scale — the one plausible candidate (`template_detail` classification) has not yet even been tested with a cheap deterministic heuristic (Experiment M-5). Architecture F (agentic LLM reasoning over large portions of the drawing set) has even less direct evidentiary support: nothing in the confirmed failure analysis (§H) is a multi-step reasoning failure — it is a grammar-arity failure and a missing-document-context failure, both of which C addresses directly and far more cheaply.

**Architecture D is not rejected outright — it is deferred and gated (`§R`, `§S`).** If, after C is in production and benchmarked (`§N`), Stage 2 (classification) or Stage 7 (detail linking) show a persistent gap that hand-authored rules and the profile mechanism can't close — e.g., truly fuzzy geometric/spatial judgments about which plan callout maps to which detail sheet — that is the specific, evidenced trigger for investing in a contextual ranker. Building D now, before that gap is measured, would mean training a model to solve a problem that has not yet been shown to survive Architecture C.

## T. Open engineering questions

Only a structural engineer or professional estimator can answer these; ordered by how much each blocks correct system behavior, not by how interesting each is.

1. **[Blocks project-rule automation, highest priority]** Does GCDC's `"W8"=W8x10` substitution actually get used on that project's own framing plans? Experiment M-3's rescan can mechanically answer "does the string `W8` appear elsewhere," but only an engineer can confirm that a matching callout is actually intended as the substitution rather than, e.g., a typo, an unrelated abbreviation, or boilerplate general-notes language carried from a firm template that doesn't apply to this specific building (`§D`, `§N` Q1 of the audit).

2. **[Blocks quantity automation for the single largest confirmed gap]** When a plate callout gives thickness only (the dominant real-world pattern, `§E`), is the fabricated size ever fully and deterministically determined by the connecting geometry — e.g., a fixed relationship to the connecting member's flange width or a standard leg length — or is it genuinely open until shop drawings are produced? If a deterministic geometric relationship exists, it could become a *computed* field rather than a permanently "missing" one; if not, the system's correct behavior is to flag, never guess.

3. **[Blocks the entire `template_detail` quantity model]** Is "a detail referenced N times → N takeoff candidates" engineering/estimating-accurate at all, or do estimators actually measure differently depending on the detail family? This audit's own external research found at least one concrete counter-pattern: continuous deck-edge conditions are conventionally measured in linear feet of edge, not discrete detail-bubble count (`§O`) — which would mean the "N references → N candidates" model may be correct for some fabricated-element families (e.g., discrete bracket details) and flatly wrong for others (e.g., a bent-plate-as-pour-stop running continuously along a slab edge) within the *same* document. This needs a family-by-family answer, not one global rule.

4. **[Blocks unit-cost accuracy directly]** Is it accurate to treat BENT PLATE / POUR STOP / CLOSURE ANGLE as one interchangeable functional role for costing purposes (as the drawings themselves treat them, `§F.3`), or do they carry meaningfully different unit costs that must stay distinguished even when a drawing note treats them as freely substitutable alternates?

5. **[Blocks connection-type interpretation on at least one real project]** Is the GCDC allowed/prohibited bent-plate-connection distinction (`BENT PLATE SHEAR CONNECTIONS` allowed vs. `BENT PLATE CONNECTIONS WELDED TO THE SUPPORTING MEMBER` prohibited, same page, `§E`) a real, meaningful bolted-vs-welded distinction the profile must preserve, or a boilerplate-notes inconsistency the source firm would want flagged back to them? The system's behavior in category 4 of `§O` depends entirely on which answer is correct.

6. **[Blocks contractor-deferred handling / allowance modeling]** When general notes defer connection design to the contractor (`§F.4`), how do estimators actually price this today — a percentage allowance against total steel tonnage, a per-connection unit allowance, a fabricator quote request cycle, or something else? This determines whether the AI system's job for AISC-Option-3-style elements (`§O`) should be "flag only, no number" or "propose a bounded allowance range with a stated basis."

7. **[Blocks whether camber/studs need dedicated cost handling]** Are camber and shear-stud count treated as directly cost-relevant line items in real estimates, or are they primarily fabrication/erection metadata carried on the beam schedule with no independent unit cost? The external research for this report found suggestive but not confidence-worthy evidence that camber specifically functions more as a revision-tracking attribute than a priced line item (`§O`) — this needs direct confirmation, not inference from vendor marketing copy.

8. **[Blocks how cleanly the AISC-option tagging in `§O`'s review workflow can be automated]** Does the ANSI/AISC 303-22 Option 1/2/3 delegated-design framework (a real, external, codified standard, `§O`) map cleanly onto how these 7 real projects' general notes actually describe connection-design responsibility, or do real project notes blend, abbreviate, or deviate from the formal AISC categories enough that automatic Option-tagging would mislead a reviewer rather than help one? This determines whether category 3 of the review UI (`§O`) can cite a specific AISC option number with confidence, or should stay qualitative ("deferred to contractor," unspecified which option).

9. **[Lower priority — refinement, not correctness]** Does the confirmed word-order asymmetry (`BENT` as a prefix on plates, a suffix on HSS/other shapes, `§F.2`) generalize to other fabrication modifiers (e.g., "curved," "cambered," "tapered") across a larger corpus, or is it specific to this sample and this modifier?

10. **[Lowest priority — affects prioritization of profile-extraction generality, not correctness of any single project]** Is the GCDC member-size abbreviation table (`§D`) — found on exactly 1 of 7 projects in this audit — a common industry practice under-sampled by a 7-project corpus, or a genuinely rare, firm-specific habit? This is the same question the audit itself raised (`§N` Q1, `§O` item 3 of the audit) and is repeated here deliberately, because it is the single most direct determinant of whether the Drawing Language Profile mechanism (Architecture C, `§Q`) is a high-value, high-frequency investment or a high-value, low-frequency one — and no amount of further reasoning from this 7-project sample alone can resolve it; it needs either a larger corpus or direct practitioner confirmation.
